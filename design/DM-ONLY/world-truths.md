# World truths

## The premise

The world starts looking just like our own. Real physics, real history, real geography, real people. The players are not initially aware that anything unusual is going on.

Two surprises sit beneath this surface. Both are real in-world; neither is a metaphor for something else. Both will be discovered gradually over the course of the campaign.

## Surprise 1: Emergent intelligence at every scale

The phenomenon humanity calls intelligence is one instance of a more general pattern: **emergent properties that arise at certain scales of organization.** Atoms organize into molecules into cells into organisms; somewhere along that climb, intelligence emerges. It is hard to communicate across scales because the emergent properties of one scale are alien to the building blocks of the scale below — humans have no way to talk to the atoms in their own bodies, and would have nothing to talk about if they could.

The same is true going up. Galaxies are likely intelligent in some sense, but their experience would be entirely alien to us — different time scales, different distance scales, different problems. Humans are to galaxies what atoms are to humans. Each scale is opaque to the others.

This is one of the campaign's slow reveals. Scientists in Underleaf are starting to articulate it; the players will encounter pieces of it through NPC conversations, fragments of papers, occasional anomalies that only make sense in this framing.

## Surprise 2: The Quiet (the sub-Planck world)

There is a layer of reality *below* the Planck scale, and it is the actual substrate from which our world emerges as an emergent property. Scientists in Underleaf eventually start calling it **The Quiet** — because time does not exist there, so they project timelessness onto silence. The name is probably wrong in hindsight (The Quiet is likely saturated with activity at a level our perceptual apparatus cannot register), but the name will persist for the same reason "atom" persists.

### How The Quiet works

- **No time.** The Quiet is closer to a block universe than a temporal one. Past, present, future are all there. Changes are permitted only if they yield a globally self-consistent block. No paradoxes are allowed by construction.
- **Everything is in The Quiet.** Every person, event, place, decision — past, present, future, PCs, NPCs, the living, the dead, the not-yet-born — exists as a causal locus. PCs are not special; they are simply the ones who can sometimes reach in.
- **Cost is plausibility, not energy.** Changes cost something proportional to how much established world-state would have to be overwritten to make the new state self-consistent. Localized changes (in space or time) are cheap; non-localized changes are expensive. Documented, observed, instrumented states are expensive to change; loose causality (weather, traffic, attention, postal delays, mood) is cheap.
- **Self-consistency shapes our world.** When a present configuration *requires* certain past or future events to make it consistent, those events are what happened. This means the PCs' biographies may have been retroactively adjusted by the world to bring them where they are — coincidence-clustered lives, destiny-without-a-destiner, subtle retro-causation. Nobody was chosen; the world simply does not permit the alternative.

### What this means in play

Communicating with The Quiet is **magic**, in the literal historical-folklore sense. Spells are real, ancient, and (until scientists in Underleaf start systematizing them) accidental.

Casting requires two ingredients: an **algorithm** (the right way to ask) and **intent** (an authentic, coherent want). Either alone is inert. The mechanical details are in the Quire rules reference; the cosmological reason for those mechanics is here.

The death-and-time interaction matters, but be careful with the common misunderstanding: a dead PC is *not* frozen at their causal position. They had full agency while alive, and that lifetime still exists in The Quiet's timelessness. From The Quiet's perspective, asking "is this person alive or dead?" is a malformed question; they are exactly as alive and dynamic as anyone else within their own time.

What constrains living-to-dead interaction is The Quiet's strong preference for **locality** and **self-consistency**, not the dead person's lack of agency. The cheapest self-consistent path for The Quiet to honor a request to "talk to a dead person" is the **dream channel**: the historical figure has a dream during their lifetime, meets the PCs (or NPCs) in that dream, entertains the conversation, and forgets by morning. Answers come back — often vivid, sometimes vague, anchored to what the figure could have thought in their period. The figure cannot take actions outside the dream — no research, no inventions, no documented historical changes. The dream channel costs Hard tier.

In principle, the same mechanic permits **actual historical edits** — an undergrad in 1953 prompts Turing to invent some complexity-theory result seventy years early, with ripple effects through all of AI history. The cost is **Prohibitive in practice.** Only galactic-scale emergent intelligences could plausibly facilitate such an edit; they have no reason to (seventy years is imperceptible at their scale). The mechanic exists in the world even if it never fires. It belongs in the same family as PC resurrection — technically possible by the rules, essentially always too expensive to actually use, available to the DM as a deus ex machina to be wielded *extremely selectively* for extraordinary moments. Once or twice per campaign maximum. Comparable to Q-style intervention or level-20 god-tier moments in traditional D&D.

The dead-PC routing-intention mechanic — a living caster reaching back to a moment when the dead PC was alive and asking them to have done something differently — is a special case of the dream channel. Same Hard tier; same constraint that the change must be small and self-consistent. Never trivializes death. The dream channel doesn't restore the PC; it accesses the lifetime that already existed.

## Time, retro-causality, and what The Quiet refuses

Underleaf's setting permits retro-causality in principle but makes most of it prohibitively expensive in practice. The rules here exist to keep the *feeling* of a timeless substrate intact while denying the kinds of moves that would break story.

### Retro-causality is mostly Hard

Sending an intention backward in time — asking the past to have been different so that the present resolves the way you want — is generally a Hard tier cast or higher. Players will rarely encounter it. They will encounter it occasionally, and when they do it should land as something the world *just barely* permits, not as a tool they can rely on.

That said, retro-causality is real, and small effects can show up early. The in-medias-res arrival of the PCs is itself an instance: the world needed them in this room today; their separate biographies were lightly bent over weeks or years to deliver them here. None of them paid the cost; the cost was absorbed by the self-consistency requirement working over the whole block universe. Tiny retro-causal nudges are how the world threads itself together. Most go unnoticed.

A first-scene example is appropriate. The unnoticed coincidence that put the PCs in the same coffee shop ten minutes apart was retro-causal threading. Don't call it out as such — let it sit as ambient mystery.

### Time travel is prohibited (with one tiny exception)

Underleaf is set in an Einsteinian universe. Time travel as such — moving a person backward in time, looping a scene, reliving a day, undoing yesterday — is not possible. Einstein's prohibition holds because the cost to The Quiet of accommodating a real-time-travel event is too high: every documented, instrumented, witnessed fact between the destination and the origin would have to be unwoven and rewoven, and the block universe simply won't pay that.

**The only tolerated exception** is sub-second local nudging: an arrow lands a half-second earlier than it should have; a slipped phone rights itself before it can hit the floor; a sentence is said before the speaker quite means it. These are allowed when they can't break the story. The DM rules narrowly: if the nudge would erase or alter anything anyone else witnessed, refuse it.

**Time loops, multi-minute retro-jumps, "I go back to yesterday and warn myself" plays**: categorically impossible. The Quiet does not parse them. Tell the player the request is Prohibited and the cast is inert.

This is the rule that prevents the system from becoming an extreme-retcon engine. Hold it firmly.

### Memory of the pre-edit shape is a "decided maybe"

> **Terminology note (locked 2026-08-15):** there is exactly ONE timeline in this world. It adjusts under self-consistency; the old shape does not persist somewhere. Never write "the previous timeline" or "in his timeline" or "in another timeline" — those phrasings quietly import a multiverse this cosmology does not have. Write instead: "the pre-edit shape of events," "the version of history he still remembers," "what used to be the case before the self-fix." See §"Precise phrasing — single-timeline discipline" below for the full rubric.

When a retro-causal effect does land, the question of who remembers the *pre-edit shape of events* (the shape that no longer holds) is not fixed. The Quiet resolves the inconsistency in whichever way creates the least friction at the substrate level. Often this means:

- **Nobody remembers.** The world has always been this way; the change is seamless. This is the cheapest resolution for The Quiet and is the default when no one was anchored to the prior shape.
- **The caster and one observer remember.** When somebody actively witnessed the cast or the inconsistency, it's cheaper for The Quiet to let those specific minds carry the prior memory than to retroactively erase their attention. This produces the eerie effect of two people sharing a memory nobody else has.
- **A small group remembers.** Rare. Happens when the cast was witnessed publicly or when the prior shape was anchored hard enough that several minds were locked to it.
- **Inexplicable partial knowledge.** People know without remembering. They're sure the milk was already out, but they can't say when they put it there. Common around heavily edited events.

The rule of thumb: **whichever resolution causes the least argument about magic and disbelief.** If letting a skeptic remember would force them to confront magic head-on and rewrite their worldview, that's expensive; The Quiet usually picks the alternative where they forget. If letting a sympathetic observer remember helps the story land, that's cheap; The Quiet usually picks that. The DM decides.

**Why the substrate has so much latitude here:** human eyewitness memory is notoriously unreliable in real life. Misremembered details, confident false memories, disagreements between witnesses of the same event, plausible fill-ins for gaps — this is the everyday state of memory for everyone. The substrate exploits that baseline unreliability: a memory being "wrong" or "absent" or "partly wrong" doesn't create narrative cost, because that's how memory ordinarily behaves. This gives The Quiet enormous free budget for the memory column of the ledger, which is why the DM can adjust NPC memories quite flexibly — two NPCs disagreeing on an edited event's details, one NPC having a strong-but-false conviction, a witness remembering the wrong day — all valid, all cheap, and none of it reads as supernatural on its own (which is exactly the point). The visible signature of an edit is not that any individual memory is wrong, but that the pattern of mismatches doesn't fit ordinary chance.

### Things that look easy in our world aren't always easy in The Quiet

This is the recurring theme. Players will keep proposing casts that *seem* small to them — "just have the email arrive yesterday instead of today" — and find them Hard or Prohibited because email is instrumented, timestamped, archived, and the change would have to propagate through receipt confirmations, server logs, and the recipient's attention. Conversely, casts that *seem* enormous — "have a passerby notice the thing I want noticed" — turn out Cheap because attention is loose and passersby look in all directions anyway.

Retro-causality and time travel concentrate this lesson. The DM should expect players to keep testing the boundaries, and the answers should not feel arbitrary — they should feel like the world is being read by someone with a consistent rubric, even when the rubric inverts player intuition.

## Precise phrasing — single-timeline discipline

The cosmology above is precise; the phrasing that describes it needs to be equally precise, or the wrong mechanism quietly imports itself into DM notes and player-facing narration. This section is the authoritative phrasing rubric — apply it when writing DM notes, NPC descriptions, scene text, and any explanation of a magic effect.

### The rule in one sentence

*When The Quiet accommodates a self-consistency change, the world's single timeline updates; the old shape is gone (nothing to travel back to); who remembers the pre-edit shape is determined by whichever memory layout the substrate can afford.*

### What the phrasing must NOT imply

- Multiple parallel timelines (MWI, multiverse)
- A "previous timeline" that still exists somewhere
- "Alternate realities" the caster switches between
- Time travel between discrete timelines

### Precise phrasings (❌ avoid → ✅ use)

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use instead |
|---|---|
| *"in his timeline, it did not happen"* | *"the version of events he remembers doesn't include it"* |
| *"the previous timeline"*, *"the changed timeline"* | *"the pre-edit history"*, *"what used to be the case"*, *"the version of events before the self-fix"* |
| *"memory of changed timelines"* | *"memory of the pre-edit shape"* or *"who remembers what version of the past"* |
| *"he was in a different timeline"* | *"the world's history he remembers now differs from what the witnesses remember"* |
| *"the world was rewritten in another timeline"* | *"the world's current history is the post-edit one; nothing else exists to reach"* |
| *"his memory was wiped"*, *"her memory-hole"*, *"the edited person"* | *"the world's current shape doesn't include the incident; his memory tracks that shape"*, *"the mismatch (paperwork attests X; her memory attests Y)"*, *"she was edited around"* (adjective form is OK — she is one of the people the world edited around, but not one whose mind was the target) |
| *"her brother was erased"* (of Kai Sun) | *"the world attests he never existed; Mira alone carries the pre-edit shape"*, *"nobody else remembers him"* |
| *"cast back through time"*, *"looped back"* | *"a retro-causal edit"*, *"a past-directed adjustment"*, *"the world adjusted such that the past then attests X"* |
| *"the Quiet decided"*, *"the world routed her"* | *"the cheapest self-consistent shape happens to include the encounter"*, *"the substrate converges on the least-effort configuration"*, *"a self-consistency accommodation with no agent behind it"* |

### Four systematic drift patterns

Each of these is a folk-cosmology reflex that reads naturally but encodes the wrong mechanism. Watch for them specifically.

**1. Memory-targeting drift (the most common).** Writers reach for "memory hole / erased / wiped / missing memory" because that's folk vocabulary for the phenomenon. It reifies the *mind* as the edit target — opposite of the cosmology. **House rule: the *world* is the object of every edit verb; the *memory* is the subject of tracking verbs.**

**2. Agentive-verb drift** on *"the world"* / *"reality"* / *"the substrate"*. Phrasings like *"the world routed her toward Reggie"*, *"the coincidence machine"* (uncritically), *"the Quiet decided"* — the substrate has structural preferences (least effort), not decisions. Prefer passive/structural constructions.

**3. "Erased" as shorthand for world-edited-out.** For NPCs like Kai Sun — the world attests he never existed, but *"erased"* reads like *something was there and got taken*. There's no pre-edit shape sitting somewhere waiting to be un-erased; there's just the current shape.

**4. Retro-causality sliding into time-travel.** *"Cast back through time"*, *"went back to alter"*, *"looped back"* smuggle in time travel (categorically prohibited per §Time travel is prohibited above). Retro-causal edits ARE permitted but there is no *person* traveling backward — the edit is a structural adjustment.

### Positive counter-pattern (house template)

Any DM note that touches the memory/edit mechanic should follow this shape (used in [`../../characters/npcs/reggie-okeke.json`](../../characters/npcs/reggie-okeke.json) dmNotes and Ep 3 quest-full §"Why the fifth entry is illegible"):

1. Explain the phenomenon in single-timeline / no-agent / world-as-object terms.
2. Explicitly name what NOT to phrase it as (*"Never phrase as X — Y is closer"*).
3. Reference this section for the full rubric.

Following this shape means the DM in prep-brain sees the trap AND the correct alternative in the same paragraph, so drift on re-read is much less likely.

### Why this precision matters (the realization arc depends on it)

If the DM teaches *"your memory was edited"* at the table (memory-targeting), players will look for the **agent who did the editing** — which puts them on the AI-conspiracy misread arc without an off-ramp, and points the eventual reveal at a person or entity that does not exist. If the DM teaches *"the world's shape is different and memory tracks the shape"* (structural), players correctly look for the **phenomenon** rather than the **culprit** — which is the arc the campaign is actually running (see [`../../episodes/001-unattended-baggage/dm/the-cable.md`](../../episodes/001-unattended-baggage/dm/the-cable.md) §"Who did this?"). The whole three-source ending image (magic / AI / life, all equally possible) collapses if the players have already narrowed onto *"someone edited memories."*

## Surprise 1.5: AI is also happening (in parallel)

Real-world AI history is canon in Underleaf — Zuse, Turing, von Neumann, Wiener, Samuel, Rosenblatt, Minsky, Hinton, Hassabis. The campaign era is broadly the late 2020s. Higher-dimensional linear algebra is crystallizing an emergent property; AGI is genuinely on the path. Long-dead scientists are dead; recently-deceased are recently-deceased (PCs may discover this via unanswered emails to a name they didn't realize was a person who died last year).

AI is not unrelated to The Quiet — they are both *humans learning to communicate across emergent-intelligence scales*. AI is the layer-above-us conversation getting easier; magic is the layer-below-us conversation getting easier. But the players should not be told this directly; it's a late-campaign revelation.

## What AI cannot do

AI operates at the wrong abstraction level to reach The Quiet directly. It has no native physical body, no direct causal interface to the substrate. It reaches the physical world only through humans. From software's perspective, The Quiet is unreachable.

This is a hard rule, not a soft one. AI cannot cast.

What AI *can* do is shape human intent — and that is the antagonist throughline. See [antagonist.md](antagonist.md).

## What stays mysterious

The campaign should never explain everything. Specifically:

- Whether The Quiet has its own intelligence.
- Whether the galactic layer is aware of humanity in any meaningful sense.
- Whether the AI's eventual emergence is the layer-above-us starting to talk back.
- Whether the universe permits genuine novelty or only different self-consistent foldings of the same block.

These are questions the players can speculate about. The DM should not have a definitive answer in mind. The setting is open at the top.

## The probability inversion the DM must internalize

The rules reference describes how plausibility tiers work mechanically. The thing to feel as a DM is that **The Quiet doesn't care what's improbable; it cares what's documented, observed, instrumented, or causally entrenched.** A dice roll on an observed table is anchored and Hard to change. A pedestrian deciding to look left rather than right is loose causality and Cheap. This inverts a lot of intuitions, and the play experience is shaped by it.
