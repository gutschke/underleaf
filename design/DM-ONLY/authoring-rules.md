# Authoring rules — the shape a standing rule takes

*Written 2026-09-06 after an audit found **~70 hard rules across seven files**,
of which **9 are genuinely load-bearing** and **one whole group of 20+ had no
release condition at all.** This file governs how rules are written from now on.
It changes nothing at the table.*

---

## 1. Two ways a proper noun enters the campaign, and both are legitimate

**The noun is never the problem. Miscategorising it is.**

| | **AUTHORED** | **IMPROVISED** |
|---|---|---|
| How it arrives | The author decided it meets the bar below | **A player asked at the table and the DM invented one on the spot** |
| Is that OK? | Yes | **Yes. The players needed a name in the moment and they got one.** |
| The bar | **Write the sentence that ends it before it enters a file.** If you cannot write the exit, it is a role, not a name. | **No bar. Improvisation cannot pass a test it has never seen.** |
| Default tier | whatever the exit sentence implies | **RECOGNISE, and terminal** |
| Where it is categorised | at authoring time | **at the post-session reconciliation, never during play** |
| Promotion | n/a | **Only by passing the authored bar afterwards** — write the exit, or leave it terminal |

> ### The failure this prevents, with the worked example
>
> **Estelle was improvised.** The DM asked for a name for a dancer who existed
> for one joke about a jacket; one was invented mid-session. **She was then given
> a file with an arc function, lane discipline and a PRIME CALLBACK flag** — the
> treatment an authored noun gets.
>
> **Nothing was wrong with the name. Everything was wrong with the category.**
> That single miscategorisation is what put a scene, a venue and a return trip on
> the schedule for a jacket joke.
>
> > **And I made the same error a second time in the same day, with Pia.** She was
> invented on the spot when the DM needed names for a dancer's late wife. She was
> then given a hook, a fire-by and a scheduled beat — **an improvised noun promoted
> to load-bearing without anyone applying the authored bar to it.** She survives
> the bar *(her exit is writable: Ep 9, the records confirm the family and explain
> nothing, retires the same session by exhaustion)* — **but the test should have
> been run before the promotion, not after.**

**Bernard was improvised too, and categorised correctly:** *"HIS JOB IS DONE.
> Do not write him a return."* Nineteen words. It is the only file in the
> directory that cannot silently grow.

## 1b. Spoken or not — the second axis, and it decides delete vs demote

**A proper noun that has been said at the table can be DEMOTED. It can never be
DELETED.** One that exists only in our own files can be deleted outright.

Demoted means: **the name stays in the record, and we stop using it in prose.**
Refer to the person by role from now on. The players keep whatever they heard;
we stop feeding it.

> **This is not pedantry. Deleting a spoken noun is a retcon**, and the campaign's
> continuity discipline is explicit that converting a mistake into canon is fine
> but wavering is not. A name the table has heard is canon whether we like it or
> not.

**Worked, from the current cleanup:**

| Noun | Spoken? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Dr Reuben Marsh** | **No** — a clipping in an Ep 2 scene file that B15 records as never having surfaced | **DELETE.** He may simply not exist. |
| **Sutter Document Services** | **Yes** — it was the payoff of a played investigation beat and is in the Ep 5 recap | **DEMOTE ONLY.** Cannot be merged away. |
| **Julián Vega** | **Yes** — said this session, and printed in the Ep 6 recap | **DEMOTE ONLY.** Becomes *"her father"* in prose; the name stays on the record. |
| **Eleanor Marsh** | Yes | **DEMOTE** — *"the archivist before Bea."* |
| **Erik Loewe** | Yes | **DEMOTE** — *"Vivian's father."* |
| **Estelle** | Yes | **DEMOTE to terminal.** Not deleted, not folded into anyone. |

**So the post-session pass acquires one specific task: list every proper noun
that was invented at the table, and categorise each one.** The default is
recognise-tier and terminal. Promoting one is a decision that must be written
down, with its exit.

## 2. Every rule takes this form

```
RULE — <one line, imperative>
PROTECTS:   the one thing that breaks if violated. One clause.
            If you cannot write it, this is a preference, not a rule.
SCOPE:      the objects it governs, enumerated. Never "and similar."
TRIGGER:    when it is consulted — per session / per design cycle / on an event.
RELEASE:    the session, act or condition at which it expires, inverts or
            narrows. MANDATORY. "None" is not permitted; a genuinely permanent
            rule must justify permanence inside PROTECTS.
RETIRES BY: exhaustion | evidence floor | expiry | n/a (permanent)
FAILURE TELL: what you would see at the table if the rule is now doing harm.
```

### Two meta-principles the template exists to enforce

**Prefer a phrasing-never to a content-never.** *"Never write 'the previous
timeline'"* cannot corner you — it governs how you say a thing, not whether the
thing may happen. *"Kai never shows up"* can. **Most content-nevers on our books
have a phrasing-never that protects the same premise.** §45's *"never append 'and
that is why it is this group'"* bans a sentence form, and the whole cosmology is
safe behind it. That is the model.

**Retiring a thread is not revealing it, and we already know two ways to do it.**

- **Retire by exhaustion** — the sources ran out. *(T7, the photograph.)*
- **Retire on the evidence floor** — the artifact does the work; no player line
  required. *(B1.)*

**Neither is a reveal.** This is the escape hatch the campaign already had and
never named, which is why every prohibition read as permanent when almost none
needed to be.

## 3. The nine load-bearing rules — permanent, and they justify it

Breaking these breaks the ending or a locked premise. **Everything else on the
books is negotiable, which is far more room than it looked like.**

1. **Structural preferences, never intentions** (§44). The generator; every other
   cosmology rule is a corollary.
2. **Kai has no cause** (§42) — *but see §4(d): as written it over-reaches.*
3. **The AI is never destroyed; the win condition is being heard.** This is Act III.
4. **The former caster is not fixable.** Fixable cost is not cost.
5. **No culprit for the gate edit; no single mentor holds the answer.** The premise.
6. **The single-timeline phrasing rubric.** Costs nothing, protects everything —
   and is the model of a safe never.
7. **Keeper vs custodian; index cards never anchor.** The artifact economy.
8. **A declined supported cast is never a veto** — and it already carries the only
   properly written escape hatch in the corpus: *"if it starts becoming a lever
   between players, drop the rule."*
9. **Archivists never meet or train predecessors** (§36).

## 4. Rules with release conditions now written in

**(a) "Default: never [reveal]"** — governs **the inciting cause only** (T11, and
T5/T8 which pay into it). **It does not travel, and it has been travelling.** It
was copied onto eight T-series hooks as house style. **The default is on naming a
WHO, not on closing a reading.** From Ep 15 it inverts for everything else: each
surviving reading retires on the evidence floor unless retiring it names a culprit.

**(b) The T-series "never (default)" as a class** — every held item now needs a
**retirement mode and a session**, not an expiry for its answer. T7 is the model.

**(c) The eight-ledger count** — *"do not correct, never confirm"* **expires the
moment the belief drives an irreversible act against a named NPC.** B27 is HOT and
this rule is currently subsidising it. It then retires by exhaustion — they acquire
enough and nothing changes — **never by an NPC saying "you don't need them."**

**(d) Kai** — *"do not re-open him"* conflates two things. **Kai never acquires a
REASON. He is not barred from acquiring a SCENE.** An appearance that supplies no
cause costs §42 nothing and pays a promise made in Ep 1.

**(e) S1's cooldown was a thermostat wired backwards** — it suspended the only
mechanism teaching *nobody intends this*, triggered by the exact behaviour the
mechanism exists to correct. **The drip never pauses. Change the SHAPE for one
session** — make the agreeing party an institution with no person in it — then
resume. Expires at Act III sub-phase 4.

**(f) "Do not ask the players yet"** — expires at the Act I→II hinge (Eps 8–9).

**(g) Farallon** — the ban is on **confirming or developing** scenes. **The name
must recur once per act as a dead end that stays dead**, or the theory goes cold
and detonates nothing.

## 5. Rules that were being obeyed as ritual

- **S2, the driver check** — its motivating condition was discharged 2026-08-30.
  Demote to a per-act check or fold into S3.
- **S4, the ledger arithmetic** — audits a count we have licensed ourselves to
  change. **Check the sentence spoken at the Ep 3 table instead**, which is the
  real invariant.
- **"Stop numbering ledgers in prose"** — justified by table speech, enforced in
  DM prose, and *"three files have already drifted apart on the numbering."*
  **The rule caused the defect it exists to prevent.** Scope to player-facing text.
- **"Every spoken line in a box, no exceptions"** — correct for findability, but
  it destroys box density as a staging diagnostic, and register monotony is the
  more dangerous failure mode.

## 6. The most dangerous rule on the books

> *"A full reveal should only fire if… at least one player has volunteered the
> theory themselves."*

**Its release condition is delegated to the players, and we have already
documented that they will not meet it** — *"they will say nothing, and quietly
collapse the entire field onto one person. They have already done it three
times."* **It fails by nothing happening**, session after session, until Ep 24
arrives and the reveal was never licensed to fire. It is the only rule in the
corpus with no escalation ladder, in a campaign that applies ladders to every
other player-gated beat.

```
RELEASE:   Ladder, checked each design cycle from Ep 15.
           (1) A player volunteers the theory -> stage it and land it.
           (2) By Ep 20, no volunteer -> an NPC offers the reading as one
               option among several, unconfirmed. A hint, not a reveal.
           (3) By Ep 22, still nothing -> the reading RETIRES on the
               evidence floor. The artifact does the work, no player line
               is required, nothing is sprung, and the rule is never broken.
RETIRES BY: evidence floor.
FAILURE TELL: three consecutive design cycles checked with no rung taken.
```

**Rung 3 is the whole point, and it is why none of this requires breaking a
rule.** We already knew how to close a thread without answering it. We had just
never written that down as the general escape hatch.
