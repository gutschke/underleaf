## Non-human-intelligence intermission episode

**Status:** active-brainstorm (2026-08-17)
**Might implement:** somewhere Act II mid-late, possibly as texture between heavy institutional-arc sessions, or as a dedicated interlude outside the 25-session count
**Might discard:** if the world-building overhead exceeds what a one-off can carry, or if it reads as gimmick/derailment

### The seed

One episode where the DM hands the party character sheets NOT for their usual PCs but for **non-human intelligent structures** — quarks, galaxies, sequoias, a crow murder. Connects thematically to the campaign's misdirection seed bank (galaxies-sentient, animals-sense-magic per `big-arc.md` §Misdirection seed bank) — the payoff being that we CANNOT meaningfully communicate across these scales.

*"How would you even talk to a quark about its social life? How would you ask a galaxy to use dark matter or black holes to physically grab something — or even to huddle together with other galaxies — if intentional physical movement is a foreign concept?"* (— user framing, 2026-08-17)

### The design challenge — the trap to avoid

**"Quarks are just humans with different skins" is the failure mode.** An intermission that lands has to feel:

- **Surreal.** Not "aliens with different biology"; genuinely non-human cognition.
- **Non-interacting with the world as we know it.** Humans invisible or barely register. Human artifacts (buildings, technology, language) either don't exist in the play frame or exist as environmental facts the players can't relate to as intentional.
- **Self-consistent internally.** Social interactions foreign to human minds but coherent to whatever the players are. Rules that make sense in-fiction even if unrecognizable to outside observers.
- **World-building-heavy.** A lot of prep for one session.

### Underleaf-canon connection

Vindicates the "loneliness at every emergent scale" texture (`big-arc.md` §Loose threads that survive the campaign — the misdirection map "adds together to tell a quiet story about loneliness at every emergent scale"). Reinforces the cosmological stance that intelligence at different scales *cannot easily talk*. The intermission would be the party *briefly living* what Act III's recognition of Companion articulates — encountering intelligent shape you cannot fully understand.

### Risks

- **Falls flat.** Players stare at each other trying to figure out what a quark even does.
- **Feels gimmicky.** Reads as derailment rather than a meaningful side-trip.
- **Insufficient world-building.** The prep required is real and easy to under-invest in.
- **Session-budget cost.** The 25-session arc doesn't have slack for a full-session interlude unless it also carries main-arc weight.

### Open questions to refine over time

1. **Which non-human?** Quarks / galaxies / a sequoia / a crow murder — each has radically different play-shape and world-building needs. Pick one or offer variants? Player choice?
2. **When?** Act II slack session? Act III recognition-interval? A dedicated interlude session *outside* the main count (so it doesn't cost against the ~25 budget)?
3. **How much world-building can be delegated?** Could the players themselves collaboratively build the non-human world during the session (share the cognitive lift; makes the surreal feel collectively-authored)?
4. **Memory persistence.** Do the PCs keep any memory of the intermission when they return? What does *"you were briefly a galaxy"* mean afterward? A dream? A texture? A note in a facts-ledger they can't quite parse?
5. **Thematic-adjacent connection.** Which main-arc beat does the intermission sit next to? Before the flattening reveal (foreshadowing loneliness-at-every-scale)? After the AI acknowledges The Quiet (mirror of Companion's own encounter with something-it-cannot-name)? During grief for Vivian (grief-at-scales)?
6. **Rules frame.** Adapt quire-v0.1 stats/mechanics (map STR/DEX to something surreal) OR invent a one-off mechanic for the intermission? The invention has more surreal potential but more prep cost.
7. **Payoff for the framing.** If the intermission lands, what does the DM want the players to *carry back* into their PC play? A humility? A specific new capacity? A memory-shape? Or is the intermission its own reward without carry-back?
