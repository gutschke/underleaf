# The Duck Has You — what happened when it was played

*Run 4, the real one, 2026-09-05. **The verdict is mixed and the mixed part is
specific.** Read this before the next edit; do not re-simulate without it.*

## What worked, and worked better than designed

**The physicality.** The table reenacted the dance — standing up, turning each
other, doing the figures. **Nothing in the design asked for that and it was the
best thing in the session.** Whatever the next version is, it must leave room for
people to get out of their chairs.

**The questions got asked.** They ended up shouting effectively all of them.
**The chaos was productive**: the answers arrived out of order and incomplete, and
the players reconstructed a comprehensive picture afterwards from their own notes.
**The reconstruction was the fun**, not the delivery.

**The gaps closed at the house.** What was still unclear went into a long scene at
Rosa's kitchen table, which finished the Q&A properly. **Design implication: the
hall does not have to land everything, and should stop trying.**

## What did not work

**The per-PC progressive arcs did not fire.** This is the central failure. Each
player's ROUND stack was written as setup → complication → payoff with a named
dancer, **and that structure did not survive contact.**

- **DO THIS cards fired at the wrong time**, out of step with where that PC
  actually was.
- **A DO THIS often played the encounter out completely** — and then later cards
  in the same stack **contradicted what had just happened**, because they assumed
  an earlier state.
- Net effect: **the arcs read as a sequence of unrelated prompts**, which is
  exactly what the arc structure was introduced to fix.

**The ADOPTED deck never came up at all.** Not once, in a full session. **Second
time this deck has failed to fire** — it also went unused in run 2. **Either it
gets a guaranteed slot or it gets cut.**

**Some answers never landed.** Notably **the birds** — Loewe's crows, the
misdirection seed — never got a response. *(Regretted at the table.)*

**The run-guide did not carry the DM through it.** Alongside the cards it did not
fire right. **Both need another editing pass and more simulated runs.**

## Where to start on the next pass

1. **Decouple the DO THIS cards from stack position.** They are the plot and they
   must be playable whenever they surface. **A card that assumes a prior card has
   already happened is the bug.** Make every green card self-contained, or make
   the ordering enforced by something the DM can actually see.
2. **Give ADOPTED a guaranteed slot or delete it.** Two failures is a verdict.
3. **Let the hall under-deliver on purpose.** The house scene closed the gaps and
   was good. **Budget for it rather than treating it as overflow.**
4. **Protect the reenactment.** It was unplanned and it carried the hour.
5. **Do not gate anything on the party doing something they were warned off** —
   see the Alaia trade, `facts-ledger.md` §46.
