# What the DM holds — Episode 3

The design principle: **every foreshadow is a promise.** Ep 3 plants a lot at once (belief-reactivation is thin; the party needs artifact-density to swing back into engagement). This doc records what each planted thread means, when it should pay off, and what breaks if the answer is left un-held. Cross-reference [`../../../design/DM-ONLY/big-arc.md`](../../../design/DM-ONLY/big-arc.md) for the arc-level frame.

Structure of each section:
- **The seed** — what the players see/hear at the table
- **What the DM must know** — the load-bearing answer
- **Fire-by** — the arc window where this needs to pay off
- **Risk if unresolved** — what breaks or feels cheap

---

## 1. The 1878 mining-crew ledger with four matched names + one illegible

**The seed** (Scene 02 §Building 7, verbatim to players): a water-damaged mining-company ledger from 1878, dog-eared to a specific page listing five members of a Bodie Mining Company crew. Four names match the PCs' modern surnames + initials (J. Reyes, M. Sun, C. Whitmore, D. Faraone). The fifth entry — occupation "physician" — has its surname portion smudged illegible. Dakota (Slot 4) left a sticky note: *"Bea has this exact roster in her index — I checked. Don't try to explain it to yourselves yet. Just SEE it."*

**What the DM must know** — the retro-causal-creation explanation:

The 1878 ledger is a **self-consistency accommodation** the world made because it required the current shape (five modern-day PCs meeting up at Bodie today for the events downstream of tonight). The cheapest self-consistent past that connects "these five here now" to the pre-edit shape needed some anchor of prior connection — an anchor that would keep coming up in future scenes without requiring further edits. A 141-year-old mining ledger with matching names does exactly that: it's a plausible-to-find, unlikely-to-be-cross-checked physical artifact that makes the shape stable.

**The 1878 miners never actually worked at Bodie.** They are not the PCs' reincarnations, ancestors, or past-life selves. They exist in the current shape's historical record because the world edited the shape to include them. Their bodies never walked; their signatures were never written by hands that lived; the ledger's ink is genuinely 141 years old (that's part of the cost — creating fresh-looking-old ink would be far more expensive than aging edits). But the CAUSAL CHAIN that would have put them there is fabricated. If a hyperskeptical scholar tried to trace J. Reyes-the-engineer through 1870s Nevada immigration records, they'd find a plausible-looking paper trail that dead-ends into records too damaged or too consumed by later fires to verify. That's the cost gradient in action — the world spent the cheap fabrication tier (records that look right but can't be independently verified), not the expensive tier (living people whose descendants would notice).

**The 5th entry is illegible because that's cheaper than making all five match.** Even one more matched name would have required more expensive fabrication (more records to touch, more chains to fabricate). Water damage is free — paper naturally deteriorates. The world chose the cheaper configuration.

**If the players do research**: any attempt to identify the illegible fifth surname produces genuinely inconclusive results — historical records are missing or damaged in ways that fit ordinary archival attrition. Do NOT let a player identify the fifth name via research; that would require the world to CREATE a specific answer, which is expensive. The illegibility is stable.

**Fire-by:** the 8-ledger network reveal in Act II (see §2 below) reframes the 1878 artifact as one node in a larger observed pattern rather than a bespoke miracle. If a player later assembles the cost-gradient rubric from Ep 2 + this artifact ("the world edited a 141-year-old physical record because it was cheaper than editing all of our memories to know each other from childhood") — treat as a magic-realization moment; grant it the "Could be. Could not be." response.

**Risk if unresolved:** if the arc ever explicitly CONFIRMS the miners existed OR CONFIRMS they didn't, the cost-gradient thesis (permanent unknowability) collapses. Hold both possibilities open forever.

## 2. The eight ledgers — Dakota's voicemail

**The seed** (Scene 05 §voicemail, verbatim): *"The ledger you found: there are eight more like it. Bea has one. I have three. There are four more. You should figure out where they are."*

**What the DM must know:**

The 8 ledgers are a distributed record system that predates Bea Ferro's index by roughly a century. Each is a physical artifact (bound ledger, index-card sleeve, or notebook) held by an "observer" role that runs in the same family as Bea's Archivist role (see Ep 2 [`../../002-the-archivists-index/dm/the-archivist.md`](../../002-the-archivists-index/dm/the-archivist.md)): a person who records but doesn't interpret coincidence-clusters. Unlike Bea's role (which is singular — one Archivist at a time, passed on death), the ledger-holders are a small **distributed network** that operates without knowing each other exists in most cases. The 8 ledgers are the currently-active nodes; there have been ~15-20 over the past 140 years, some retired, some lost, some absorbed into Bea's index.

Each ledger holds a rolling record of "self-consistency-fingerprint" events its holder has witnessed — coincidences that don't fit ordinary chance. The Bodie ledger sat in a specific outbuilding (Building 7) because it was written and maintained on-site by the ledger-holder of that generation, who was a maintenance worker at the Standard Mill in the late 19th century and passed it down through the wartime annex staff (see §3). It stayed in Building 7 because that's where its keeper died in 1948, and nobody knew to retrieve it.

**Locations of the four remaining ledgers** (the DM should have specific answers for later reveals):

- **Palo Alto, in a private library** owned by the estate of a mid-20th-century AI researcher (not a household name; a peer of Wiener's). Held by an executor who inherited it without understanding what it was.
- **Berkeley, in the flat file of a librarian at Bancroft Library** who catalogued it as an "anomalous ephemera collection" in 1979 and has occasionally added new entries when she personally witnesses something. She IS a former caster (see Act II's Former Caster invariant); the ledger is her outlet for the pattern-sensing she can no longer act on.
- **A rented storage unit in San Bernardino County**, part of a lot from an intestate estate that a hoarder-collector bought at auction in 2013. He has no idea what he owns. Ledger is at the bottom of a stack of unread pulp fiction.
- **In a Buddhist temple's storeroom in the Central Valley**, given as a gift by a visiting scholar in the 1980s who left no explanation. The monks preserve it out of politeness without reading it.

**Dakota's three ledgers** (per his voicemail, "I have three"): pieced together from the Sacramento self-storage lead (see §3). His three are: the Palo Alto one (his in-fiction acquisition method: he tracked the executor and convinced her to lend it), a partial one recovered from the estate itself, and a fragment of a fifth ledger that's really a set of loose annotated index cards (proto-Bea).

**Fire-by:** the ledger network is the physical bridge from Bea Ferro's local Bay Area role to the larger caster-network arc (Mira Chen and beyond) in late Act I / early Act II. When Mira introduces the network, part of what she can offer is *"we don't have a guild hall; we have ledgers"* — the ledger holders overlap with but are not identical to the casters. Ledger-holding is a mostly-passive skill; casting is separate. Some ledger-holders are casters; most are not.

**Risk if unresolved:** the number "eight" is a promise. If Act II never engages the ledger network, the party will feel Dakota's voicemail was a cheap tease. Bake ledger discoveries into the natural investigation cadence of Act II — at least three of the four remaining should be found or referenced by the party's own work.

## 3. How does Dakota know? — the off-screen Sacramento thread

**The seed** (Scene 05 §DM placeholder + voicemail): Dakota is off following a lead from Bea's index to a Sacramento self-storage unit paid up-front through 2035 by an LLC that dissolved in 1998. Inside: three of the eight ledgers, hand-annotated in the margins with a hand not Bea's. One margin note names a person Dakota has now driven to look for.

**What the DM must know:**

The self-storage unit belongs (belonged) to a woman named **Eleanor Marsh**, born 1922, died 1997. Her name doesn't appear on the LLC (the LLC was a discretionary trust set up by her lawyer to keep the storage unit paid indefinitely). She was NOT a caster, but she was a ledger-keeper who inherited the role in 1965 from her uncle. Her lifetime overlapped with several ledger-holders and she cross-annotated their work — that's the "hand not Bea's" Dakota sees in the margins. She was a research librarian at Sacramento State until retirement in 1985.

**The margin note names Mira Chen.** Not the peer-generation caster from Act I — a different Mira Chen. Or, more precisely: Eleanor Marsh's notes reference *a Mira Chen who wasn't born yet when Eleanor wrote the notes.* Eleanor's margin says: *"Ask Mira Chen — she'll know what this cluster is."* The note is dated 1979.

**This is the retro-causal fingerprint applied to Act II's discovery arc.** Eleanor's ledger — written in ordinary human handwriting on ordinary 1979 paper — includes references to Mira Chen and to a small cluster of Bay Area coincidence-events that hadn't happened yet in 1979 but happen in the campaign's 2020s. That's cost-gradient in action: the cheapest self-consistent path from Dakota's Ep 3 arrival at Bodie to the party meeting Mira in Act II routes through Eleanor's 1979 notes now pointing at Mira. The margin annotation was ALWAYS THERE (from Eleanor's POV, she wrote it because it felt right to write; she couldn't have said why; she was, on reflection, a soft-network Bea-adjacent figure). The world edited the shape so Eleanor's writing was informed by what would need to be there when Dakota looked in 2027.

Dakota drove to look for Mira Chen based on the margin note. He hasn't found her yet as of Ep 3's close. That's the Ep 4 opening.

**Fire-by:** Ep 4 opening at latest. Dakota's return + his find IS the Ep 4 arc kick.

**Risk if unresolved:** Dakota's absence and voicemail loading the whole session with mystery means Ep 4 MUST open with his return. Deferring past Ep 4 makes the whole Ep 3 setup feel like a stall.

## 4. The Field Annex 7 plaque — wartime 1942-1948

**The seed** (Scene 02 §Building 7): a brass plaque above the door on the inside face, ducked to see: *"Field Annex 7 — WD/DOI — 1942-1948 — Access Restricted"*. WD = War Department. DOI = Department of the Interior. The visitor-center brochure has a line about "select outbuildings requisitioned by federal agencies between 1942 and 1948 for classified purposes; records remain sealed."

**What the DM must know:**

The annex was a small (six-person max at peak) satellite site of a wartime information-theory / early-computing research effort that used remote locations to test specific hypotheses about signal detection and coincidence-frequency in physical systems. Real-world analogues: Bell Labs' wartime cryptography work, MIT Rad Lab, Bletchley Park's remote outstations. The Bodie annex was a probability-and-coincidence-instrumentation site — the researchers there set up mechanical counters and photographic timers on natural events (weather anomalies, mining-machinery failures, wildlife counts) and looked for statistical departures from expected distributions.

**They found some.** Not enough to publish; enough to note. One of the researchers was **Norbert Wiener** — for one summer, 1943, on a two-week working visit. Wiener was in Mexico that spring (per real history), returned to MIT for the summer, and — per the campaign's alt-history bridge — took a two-week teaching-and-observation trip to Bodie at the invitation of the annex lead. He didn't stay long. He wrote nothing publicly about it. But his private notes (which he burned before his death per real history) included a page about "the Bodie summer" and what he'd seen there.

Wiener is one of the campaign's dream-channel targets in Act III (per big-arc.md Act III §4, "The Quiet named"). The wartime annex plaque is the seed that lets Wiener's dream-channel scene be richer: when the PCs eventually contact him, they can ask about the annex specifically. He will say (in dream): *"We were looking for coincidence-signatures. We found some. I thought they were measurement artifacts for the rest of my life. I was wrong."* The annex plaque today is the DM's promise that this dream-channel moment will land with weight rather than as a lore-dump.

**Other pioneers who touched the annex** (available for the DM if a player asks):
- **Claude Shannon** (visited once, 1946, on the way home from a Los Alamos consultation; stayed one night; wasn't shown the interesting data)
- **John von Neumann** (never visited; corresponded with the annex lead; his correspondence is one of the sealed items in the National Archives referenced by the ranger)
- The **annex lead** himself — an obscure figure named **Dr. Erik Loewe** — is available as a Marsh-tier Lore-Reader NPC if a deep-layer player chases the archive. Loewe died in 1961.

**Fire-by:** the wartime annex thread is a slow burn. Its main fire is in Act III sub-phase 4 (Wiener dream-channel). It can also fire at the Marsh-seed level in mid Act II if a player is chasing "the wartime lineage" of the coincidence-noticers. Do not fully fire before Act II.

**Risk if unresolved:** if Wiener's dream-channel scene in Act III references the annex without prior planting, it will feel like DM fiat. Ep 3's plaque is precisely the pre-planting that makes the Act III moment earn its weight.

## 5. Ranger Alaia Vega

**The seed** (Scene 02 §the ranger, various DM notes): young park ranger at Bodie, practiced-friendly, deflects on the annex brochure line, doesn't recognize Dakota by name. Late-Q3 suspicious beat: she notices the stove is lit + the dry wood pile that wasn't there when she checked in the morning; says *"I'm glad you got warm"* meaningfully. Q4 close: hands each PC a small park feedback-QR-code card — with the DM-note that the QR codes are DIFFERENT for each PC. Character-sheet DM note says she's "not an NPC of note" but has recurring-asset framing.

**What the DM must know:**

Ranger Vega is a **former caster** (see big-arc.md Act II §"Emotional landmark: the former caster" — the invariant is that the campaign refuses to fix them). She lost her ability roughly seven years ago at age 22, after a period of intensive uncontrolled casting during a personal crisis. She doesn't remember being a caster, but she remembers the FEELING of noticing patterns, and she never lost the pattern-noticing itself — just the ability to act on it. Being a park ranger in a remote place suits her because the coincidence-density is very low; the noise she can't act on stays quiet out here.

She recognizes Dakota's message pattern from **her mother's ledger** — her mother was the Bancroft librarian who holds Ledger 6 (see §2). Alaia grew up around the ledger; she doesn't know what it is; she knows the shape of the writing and the way coincidences cluster around certain events. Dakota's cryptic-invitation-photo has the shape of a ledger event. That's why she was suspicious.

**The QR codes.** Not sinister. They're actually links to the park's genuine feedback form — but personalized per visitor per Vega's private practice. She's been quietly tagging visitors who visibly notice things (a subtle Insight test she runs), assigning each a personal QR-code that maps to her private log. The DM should NEVER reveal this openly; if a player scans a code, it goes to the ordinary park feedback form (which is a real form). Only if the party revisits Bodie and Alaia trusts them do they learn the codes are her private tracking system. **This is a mid-Act-II reveal, not now.**

**She is not with the AI institution. She is not with any faction. She is her own quiet observer,** part of the informal ledger-adjacent culture that surrounds ledger-holders. If the party finds her mother's ledger in Berkeley later, they can trace back to Alaia and get a warmer welcome on a return visit.

**Fire-by:** mid Act II, alongside other former-caster material. Alaia is the DM's "warm" version of the former-caster archetype — for tables who need the emotional landmark to be less bleak than the single-encounter Act II former caster.

**Risk if unresolved:** the QR-code detail was a substantial planted mystery. If the party notices they got different codes and asks — the DM must be ready with the answer above (the codes go to the ordinary form; her private tracking is separate). If Alaia never recurs, the QR-code detail feels cheap. Consider promoting her to a recurring texture NPC if the party engages with her at all.

## 6. SFPD Missing Persons — {{pc:1}}'s father, the ring-close phone call

**The seed** (Scene 05 §final read): as the party drives out of Bodie, {{pc:1}}'s phone buzzes: the SFPD Missing Persons detective who's had his father's file open for two years is calling back for the first time in six months.

**What the DM must know:**

The detective — **Detective Camila Ortiz** — has news, but not what {{pc:1}} might hope for. She'll say (paraphrased): *"We got a possible sighting from a bank surveillance camera in Fresno two weeks ago. Older Latin man, right build, right approximate age. Face was mostly turned away. Our facial-recognition ran it and got 62% confidence, which for our system is a maybe — we don't act on it and we don't ignore it. I wanted you to know we're keeping the file open. If anything else surfaces, you'll be the first call."*

**What she doesn't know** (and the DM holds):

{{pc:1}}'s father is alive. He is not in Fresno; he is not the person on the camera (that person is genuinely a coincidental lookalike, a retired schoolteacher with no connection). {{pc:1}}'s father's disappearance is genuine — he vanished for reasons {{pc:1}} does not yet know — and he is currently living under a different name in **Nevada City** (about an hour from Bodie, as it happens; not a coincidence in the cost-gradient sense — the world routed Bodie because of adjacency to his current location, one more small self-consistency cost). He has been alive but hidden for two years. The reason he vanished: he was quietly informed by an old colleague that his federal-red-team-adjacent past (he was telecom-adjacent for the same kind of contract that now employs {{pc:1}}) was catching up to him, and he chose to disappear rather than either fight the system or explain to his son why he had to. He watches {{pc:1}} from a distance; he knows {{pc:1}} is a hacker; he assumes {{pc:1}} is safer without him.

**When and how this fires:** {{pc:1}}'s father recurs as an active thread in mid Act II (session 10-12 range) when the party has enough investigative momentum + connection to the caster/observer world that his story becomes findable. He is NOT a caster; he is a person the AI-antagonist arc's institutional predecessors made "quietly move to Nevada City" without knowing what they were doing. His story is a small, human parallel to the campaign's larger AI-flattening arc — one man's life shaped by the same optimization pressure that later shapes millions.

**{{pc:1}} and his father reunite** in late Act II or early Act III, ambiguously. The father will not have answers to what {{pc:1}}'s life has become; {{pc:1}} will find him living a small quiet life; the emotional beat is not resolution but relief — the missing-person case can be closed, but the years cannot.

**Fire-by:** Act II mid-arc for the discovery; Act II late / Act III early for the reunion. Do NOT resolve tonight or in Ep 4. The SFPD call in Ep 3 close is deliberately inconclusive.

**Risk if unresolved:** {{pc:1}}'s father is a personal thread that grounds the arc's macro theme in a family-level story. If never paid off, the character's arc feels incomplete. Bake into the mid-Act-II investigation cadence.

## 7. Dakota's photograph — brown-edged, cropped faces, clapboard buildings

**The seed** (set-piece §Dakota's absence): *"Three weeks ago, the group chat woke up. {{pc:4}} — [player context] — dropped a photograph in the chat with no caption. Old, brown at the edges. A row of clapboard buildings on a dirt street. Two figures in the foreground with their faces cropped."*

**What the DM must know:**

The photograph is from the Bancroft Library ledger (§2, Berkeley ledger held by Alaia's mother). Dakota found it via Bea (the intermediate step of his research). The photo shows a Bodie street in ~1935 (verifiable via the buildings' known repair timelines). The two figures with cropped faces are the ledger-holder of that generation (an unremarkable local) and a visiting Berkeley graduate student who was there for a completely unrelated reason. Neither figure is anyone the campaign will later reveal as important. **The cropping is not sinister** — the ledger-holder cropped it themselves, in a private-notebook-style habit of not photographing faces.

Dakota dropped it in the chat as a *shape* — he wasn't asking the party to solve it; he was signaling "look, there's a THING here." It worked. It got their attention.

**Fire-by:** never fully. The photo is stage-setting, not a puzzle. If a player invests significant time on identifying the figures, direct them toward Bancroft (Ledger 6) as the archival source and let the trail dead-end there.

**Risk if unresolved:** almost none — the photograph functioned as evocative texture, not a real Chekov. If the party ignores it, that's fine. If they chase it, the trail to Bancroft becomes a useful lead-in for that ledger.

## 8. The Ocean Beach teenagers — recurring texture NPCs

**The seed** (per play-state memory Ep 2): the Ep 2 played variant had a group of teenagers at an Ocean Beach bonfire; the party bought them booze; the teenagers pointed out the anti-static bag's chain-of-custody stopped at SFO; **the teenagers were added to a second group chat.**

**What the DM must know:**

They're recurring texture NPCs, not plot-critical. They ARE, however, a useful group-chat presence for the DM to occasionally seed background context (news of a Bay Area coincidence event; a "did you see this?" screenshot). Give them names on the fly (5 teenagers, 15-17 years old, mixed friend group). One of them may — in later Acts — become a very small Bay Area caster (Phase 1, never realized) as a background-network expansion signal. But that's optional and never load-bearing.

**Fire-by:** whenever the DM wants a low-stakes background beat. Occasional group-chat pings; a "we're near you if you need something" moment during a Bay Area investigation; texture.

**Risk if unresolved:** none. They exist to remind the party they have a small kind Bay Area community they helped, without asking anything back.

## 9. Yui's ongoing situation

**The seed** (built up across Ep 2 + implicit continuation): Yui almost got fired over the bag-hold incident; corporate couldn't prove wrongdoing; her memory doesn't include the incident; the mismatch is quietly costing her the purser application she'd been working toward.

**What the DM must know:**

Yui doesn't feature in Ep 3 directly. She continues to work, continues to distrust her own mind, does not get the purser role in the promotion cycle (subtly denied without explanation). Her family life is uneven — Mei has been sick again; Inez is trying to be supportive but tired. This is Act II background that the party can revisit if they want to check on her (highly recommended — the DM should nudge the caregiver PC to check in on Yui at least once in Act II).

**Fire-by:** ongoing Act II texture. Optional Act II scene if the party checks in. Not a fresh plot; a maintained thread.

**Risk if unresolved:** if Yui is not revisited even once in Act II, the "cost paid by people who never know they paid it" theme (per Ep 2 Scene 3) doesn't land its full weight. The party's ongoing awareness of Yui is what makes the theme *stick*.

## Discipline reminder

Every answer above is what the DM **holds** — not what the DM **shares**. The players discover the answers (or don't) through play. The DM's job is to keep the answers coherent so that when they DO emerge, they land with weight instead of feeling like DM fiat. If a player pushes toward an answer this doc says fires in Ep 5, respond with *"could be, could not be — what does your character do?"* and log the push for future callback.

**Never fully confirm any answer above at the table until its scheduled fire-by.** Confirmations before that point deflate the payoff.
