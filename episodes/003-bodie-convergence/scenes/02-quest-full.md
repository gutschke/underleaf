# Episode 3 — Quest: *The Arrested Town*
*Bodie State Historic Park. Late winter afternoon. The road out is closed.*
*Target time: ~75-90 min of quest play after the ~17 min set-piece opening.*
*Total session budget: 90-120 minutes. Buffer built in.*

---

## Arc position (DM's private map)

Where this episode sits in the campaign shape:
- **Arc-beat one:** *pattern-is-older-than-the-PCs.* Ep 2 planted; Ep 3 corroborates with a physical artifact (the 1878 mining-crew ledger).
- **Arc-beat two:** *first-witnessed-accidental-cast.* Ep 2 was the first cast the group experienced (Yui / footage / lights-out). Ep 3 is the first one the players *watch each other do* — the "we all saw that" beat.
- **Arc-beat three seeded:** *wartime annex* — a foreshadow of the pioneer-generation thread (Turing / Wiener / Rosenblatt cameo material). Fires much later. Ledgered in the Chekov tracker.
- **Not this session:** the realization beat (Phase 1 → Phase 2 transition). Save for Ep 4-5.

## Scene skeleton — 4 scenes, ~75 min quest

| Scene | Target | What must land |
|---|---|---|
| Q1. **The Ranger's Warning** | 10 min | Storm-trap; visitor-center scan; the brochure line lands with everyone; {{pc:4}}'s cryptic message escalates |

## Q1. The Ranger's Warning (~10 min)

**Where we are:** Standard Mill, four PCs in a circle. {{pc:4}}'s "Yes" text has just landed. Signal is gone. Ranger Alaia Vega has walked past.

**What happens:**
- Ranger Vega comes back to them. She's practiced-friendly but her body language has changed. She tells them a **fast-forming winter storm** is dropping the last passable window in about ninety minutes; if they leave now they'll make Bridgeport by 4pm. If they stay past the window, the road will close and they'll be spending the night in Bodie. She has a small cabin ("the tour guides' bunkhouse — it's rough but it's warm"). She'd prefer them to leave.
- If asked about the **brochure's federal-annex line** — she deflects: *"That's the line everyone reads twice. Above my pay grade. Some of the buildings had a war-era chapter, records aren't public, I don't ask."* She's not lying. She just doesn't want to be a person who talks about it.
- If asked about **{{pc:4}} by name** — she doesn't recognize the name. No one by that name registered today or yesterday. She checks her clipboard.
- **The players decide:** stay or go.

**Expected outcome:** They stay. {{pc:4}}'s message + the sudden signal drop + the ledger of "we're all here because he sent us" makes leaving feel like giving up. If they *do* try to leave, run the storm-catching-them-on-the-road version: the car makes it four miles down the washboard before the visibility drops to zero and they have to hike back to town. Same downstream: they end up in the ghost town for the storm.

**DM lines to have ready:**
- Vega's brochure deflection: *"That's the line everyone reads twice. Above my pay grade."*
- Vega's {{pc:4}}-search: *"Whitmore, Whitmore... no. Nobody by that name. You sure this was today?"*
- Storm coming: *"You know how the eastern slope drops a system in an hour. That's the shape of this one. I need to close the road at three."*

**Scene budget:** 10 min. Compressible to 6 min if players decide fast.

## Q2. Building 7 & the Annex (~25 min)

**Where we are:** the ghost town, mid-afternoon, still light. Wind picking up. The PCs are looking for {{pc:4}}'s "building 7."

**Bodie proper** — key locations to work with (verified against real park layout, but the DM should feel free to invent details):
- **The Standard Mill** — the big landmark, the mining-processing structure on the south side; large, echoing, rusted equipment inside
- **The Miner's Union Hall** — center of town, one of the more intact buildings, wood floors, will figure in Q3
- **The Assay Office** — small, higher on the slope, records-adjacent, good scene for {{pc:3}}
- **The Methodist Church** — cold stone, near the cemetery; almost never open to visitors
- **The Firehouse** — small, red-painted, a bell tower still on it
- **Rows of clapboard residences** — most locked, some open to visitors, texture

**"Building 7"** — the DM decides which of these {{pc:4}} meant, or invent an unnamed outbuilding on the periphery. My recommendation: **an unassuming clapboard structure at the east edge of the residential rows, marked "Building 7" on the very old surveyors' map** the visitor center gives out, but *unmarked* on the modern brochure. It's boarded up. The boards are not from 1880 — they're rough plywood, aged but industrial, added later. There's a rusted padlock. The lock is old but not that old — a Yale from probably the 1950s.

**Getting in** — three paths, in escalating cost:
1. **Ask Ranger Vega for the key** — she'll refuse politely (*"That's not on the visitor loop. Sorry."*). If the players Influence-check ({{pc:4}}'s not present; the strongest CHA at the table is {{pc:2}} at +1, or {{pc:5}} with the +2), a **10+ hit** gets her to admit the key is in the visitor-center safe and she'd need her supervisor's sign-off which she'd get *tomorrow*, not today. **7-9 partial:** she says she'll walk them past it later but they can't go in. **Miss:** she becomes wary and starts checking on them more often.
2. **Pick the lock** — {{pc:3}} has "can pick a Kwikset lock in under a minute" as a tag; the Yale is a step up. Subterfuge check with the tag: **10+** in about two minutes with no witness; **7-9** in about two minutes with the lock damaged (evidence they were there); **Miss** she can't get it open and the lock is now slightly gouged.
3. **Force it** — {{pc:5}} with the crowbar he keeps in his truck (STR + Action + a tag). Fast and loud.

**Inside Building 7** — a single room, twenty feet square, packed with:
- **Dust everywhere.** Recent footprints in the dust are {{pc:4}}'s — a size 11 hiking boot, one set in and one out, days old.
- **Old wooden shelving on three walls**, mostly empty. On one shelf: a **stack of ledgers**, oldest at the bottom, most recent at top of the pile is dated 1948.
- **A brass plaque above the door on the inside face** — you have to duck to see it: *"Field Annex 7 — WD/DOI — 1942-1948 — Access Restricted"*. WD = War Department. DOI = Department of the Interior. Both would have been active labels in the 1940s.
- **The ledger {{pc:4}} wanted them to see** is on the top of the pile, dog-eared to a specific page. It's a mining-company ledger dated **1878**, listing the members of a Bodie Mining Company crew that worked the Standard Mill for six months that year. The dog-eared page lists **five names**, but the paper has been water-damaged along the right edge:
  - *J. Reyes* — engineer
  - *M. Sun* — clerk
  - *C. Whitmore* — foreman
  - *D. Faraone* — laborer
  - a fifth entry — occupation "physician" — with the surname portion blotted illegible; only the initial visible: *__ ______y or ______r*, hard to read. Ink smudged with what looks like coffee, decades ago.
- {{pc:4}} has left a **sticky note** on the ledger page in his handwriting: *"I know how this looks. It's real. Bea has this exact roster in her index — I checked. Don't try to explain it to yourselves yet. Just SEE it. — C."*

**Do not run this as a jump-scare.** Let it *sit*. Read the four legible names slowly. Let a beat go. Then mention the fifth line with the smudged surname. Let the players react. Let them look at each other. Let someone say *"is this a joke?"* and let another one say *"no."* {{pc:2}}'s player will likely notice the physician entry is smudged and demand to try to read it. Let her try. She can't. It stays illegible.

**Why the fifth entry is illegible (DM only):** the self-consistency accommodation has a *cost gradient* — the single-timeline shape in which five 1878 miners had matching modern names is more expensive than the shape in which four do. The substrate converged on the cheaper shape; that's why the fifth entry is smudgeable. This is the cost-gradient lesson from Ep 2, restated in a physical artifact. Do NOT tell the players any of this today. *(Cosmology precision: there is one timeline; it currently attests four matching names and one illegible one; there is no "other timeline" where five match — that shape was more expensive and the world settled on the cheaper alternative. See [`../../../design/DM-ONLY/world-truths.md`](../../../design/DM-ONLY/world-truths.md) §"Precise phrasing — single-timeline discipline".)*

**If {{pc:2}}'s player guesses "Iyer" from the smudge:** *"You look. Cold light through the boards, the ink is genuinely gone in that spot. You can see it starts with a vowel — an 'I' or an 'E' — and the last letter is what looks like an 'r' or 'n' or maybe an 'y' bent under the tear. You couldn't testify to any of it. You couldn't rule any of it out."* Do not confirm the match; do not deny it. Let her keep the guess as a guess.

**The wartime seed** — the plaque + the ledger dates (1878 mining, then 1942-1948 restricted use) is the foreshadow. Do NOT explain the connection tonight. If a player asks *"why would a mining ledger from 1878 be in a WW2 restricted annex?"* — that is the RIGHT question. Bea Ferro will know part of the answer in a future session. {{pc:4}}'s off-screen thread will bring the other part.

**Expected outcome:** The party holds a photograph of the ledger page ({{pc:2}} has a smartphone; even with no signal, the camera works). They exit Building 7. It's snowing now. The storm has beaten Ranger Vega's ninety-minute window by half an hour.

**Scene budget:** 25 min. Compressible to 15 min if players hit the ledger fast and there's little Bea-Ferro-callback riffing; extensible to 35 min if the players want to inventory the whole building.

## Q3. The Storm & the Cast (~25 min)

**Where we are:** Blizzard conditions. Visibility twenty feet, dropping. The PCs run for shelter. Ranger Vega has already taken the visitors' car of two earlier tourists ({{pc:1}} saw them leave) into the bunkhouse on the far side of town — she is not immediately accessible. The Miner's Union Hall is the nearest large intact building; the door is locked but the lock is a museum-grade replica the park keeps loose for tour access, and the padlock is undone (the ranger opens it every morning). They can get in.

**Inside the Miner's Union Hall:**
- Big central room, wood floors, tall ceilings, cold. A wood stove in the corner but no wood cut. Dust motes in the fading light.
- **The temperature drops fast.** Within thirty minutes it's below freezing inside. The PCs are wearing what they brought for a ghost-town walk in winter, which is not enough for an unheated wooden hall with no stove.
- **Someone needs to solve the cold problem.** Options: burn the interpretive-panel wood (destructive, guilt), find wood outside in a blizzard, huddle under coats (dangerous), or something else.

**The accidental cast** — this is the load-bearing beat of the session. The DM picks *who* casts based on table dynamics. Rank order of best candidates:
1. **{{pc:2}}** — highest thematic fit (body/care domain, wants to keep the group warm and alive; her instinct will be to solve the cold). If she describes wanting to warm the room, warm one of the PCs, or "just get us through this," grant a Free-tier cast: the stove is suddenly lit (dry wood inside, matches on the mantel — none of which were there when they came in), OR the wind outside quiets for the next hour, OR someone's shivering just stops.
2. **{{pc:3}}** — her domain is memory/identity. Less direct fit for cold, but a good candidate for "she remembers where the wood used to be kept" — reality accommodates by making that memory *true.*
3. **{{pc:1}}** — his domain is systems/information. A stretch for cold but if he tries to fix the electrical or start the (long-decommissioned) stove somehow, allow it.
4. **{{pc:5}}** — his domain is posture/presence. Least fit; he'd need to be doing something more social than practical for a cast to land in his lane.

**Whoever casts:** it's Free-tier, no roll, no stress. But **the other three watch it happen.** The stove flames up on its own. A pile of dry wood is on the floor near it that they *did not see there* thirty seconds ago. Or the wind outside stops mid-howl. Something concrete. Something that would be uncanny even to a person who wasn't there for Ep 1.

**The argument** — this is what eats the next 15 minutes and it's what the session is really *for*. The players will:
- Try to explain it away. Let them. *(DM only: rationalization is the cheapest configuration the substrate can settle into for a witness's memory — no smaller adjustment is needed. That's why belief drifts back toward "it was nothing" over time. The world doesn't "reward" the rationalization in an agentive sense; it's just where the least-effort shape lands. Do NOT use this phrasing at the table.)*
- Fight about whether it happened. {{pc:2}} (curious to weirdness) will lean into it. {{pc:3}} (suspicious) will hold back. {{pc:5}} (curious to weirdness) will want to test it. {{pc:1}} (suspicious) will look at his phone every eight seconds.
- Ask the caster to *do it again.* The DM must refuse to grant a repeat cast. The world does not perform on demand — the accidental phase means the caster cannot summon effects intentionally. Whoever casts, when asked to repeat, tries and it does not work. This is the FIRST time they experience the *asymmetry* between accidental luck and willed magic. This is the seed of the realization beat coming in Ep 4-5.

**DM lines to have ready:**
- *"You're sure it was cold when you came in?"* (Vega, later, warmly innocent)
- *"Try to make it happen again."* (from a player) → *"They try. Nothing. The stove is still going but that's just fire now. They can't do it."*
- If a player says the word "magic": *"Could be. Could not be. What does your character do?"*
- If a player says "AI" or "conspiracy": **same line — do NOT affirm.** *"Could be. Could not be. What does your character do?"* The wrong-right theory should be *fed by their own reasoning*, not *validated by the DM.* An affirming line from you tips your hand.

**Silent-player-firewall reminder:** DO NOT SAY "MAGIC" AS THE DM. Not once. The word "cast" only appears in this document (DM-facing); at the table you say "she reached for the stove" or "he felt the wind ease." Even after the group *guesses* magic, hold *"could be, could not be."*

**Optional: Ranger Vega's suspicious beat** — if the session has slack, have Vega arrive at the Miner's Union Hall late in Q3 to check on them. She notices the stove is lit. She notices there was no wood cut when she checked in the morning. She looks at the pile of dry wood on the floor. She looks at the PCs. She does not ask. She says *"I'm glad you got warm."* And she means it, and she has just added them to a mental file she doesn't tell anyone about.

**Scene budget:** 25 min. Not compressible below 20 without losing the cast argument. Expandable to 35 if the players are riffing hard on the cast.

## Q4. The Road Out (~15 min)

**Where we are:** The storm has cleared as fast as it came. It's evening, the sun is very low, the road is passable if they leave now. Vega is efficient again; she wants them out.

**What must happen:**
1. **A voicemail from {{pc:4}}.** As their phones' signal returns, all four get the same push notification: one voicemail, from {{pc:4}}, timestamped ~40 minutes ago. Play it:

   > *({{pc:4}}'s voice, wind in the background, sounds like he's outdoors somewhere else)* *"So I don't know if you found it. But I found the other thing — you know, the OTHER thing. It's not what I thought it was, and it's bigger. I'm not coming back for a while. Don't come looking. But — hey — the ledger you found: there are eight more like it. Bea has one. I have three. There are four more. You should figure out where they are. See you when I see you. Sorry to have vanished. It'll be worth it."* *(end)*

   **DM placeholder — what {{pc:4}} actually found (for continuity, hold until Ep 4):** {{pc:4}} followed a note in Bea's index to a self-storage unit in Sacramento paid up-front through 2035 by an LLC that dissolved in 1998. Inside: three of the eight ledgers, hand-annotated in the margins with a hand not Bea's. One margin note names a person {{pc:4}} has now driven to look for. Do not commit publicly to this until Ep 4; it's the placeholder answer so you don't have to improvise if a player pushes on where {{pc:4}} went.

2. **A parting from Ranger Vega.** She walks them to their cars. She hands each of them a small **printed card** with a park logo and a QR code: *"Feedback form — we appreciate it."* Nothing suspicious about it individually; it's a real park thing. But she gives them each one, individually, with eye contact. **The DM notes:** the QR codes are different for each PC. This is a seed for future — Vega is not who she says she is. Do not resolve this session.

3. **{{pc:1}}'s father** — as they get in their cars, {{pc:1}}'s phone buzzes. It's the SFPD Missing Persons detective who's had his father's file open for two years, calling him back for the first time in six months. {{pc:1}} can pick up or let it go to voicemail. Either way, the ring closes the session. **This is a Chekov re-seed** — I planted it in the character sheet, now it lightly re-fires. Do not resolve.

**Final read-aloud (session close):**

> *"You drive out of Bodie together in three cars, one behind the next, headlights on, the road ahead of you clean and cold. You have a photograph of a 141-year-old ledger with your names on it. You have a voicemail from a friend who is not answering his phone. You have a folded feedback card in each of your pockets. And somewhere behind you, past the last ridge of the eastern slope, the shape of the ghost town is disappearing into the last light of the day."*
>
> *"You are being watched. You do not know it. And what is watching you would have a difficult time explaining what watching means."*
>
> *"End of session three."*

**Scene budget:** 15 min. Compressible to 10 min if the table is tired.

## What the players walk away with

- **Corroboration of the pattern** — four names, 1878, same as theirs; a fifth entry smudged to illegibility (physician, initial-only). Not deniable at the table (they'll try, they'll fail).
- **A witnessed cast** — they *saw* one of them do something impossible. They can't unring that bell.
- **A missing friend with a plan** — {{pc:4}} is doing something. He knows what he's doing (or at least sounds like he does). The next session has a hook already.
- **Physical artifacts** — the photograph of the ledger; the Building 7 sticky note in {{pc:4}}'s writing; a feedback card each.
- **New questions:**
  - *"Are there eight more ledgers?"* (per {{pc:4}}'s voicemail — yes, there are)
  - *"Who is Ranger Vega really?"* (a much later reveal)
  - *"What was the Field Annex 7 doing between 1942 and 1948?"* (Ep 6-8 payoff)
  - *"Where is {{pc:4}}?"* (Ep 4 opening)

## What the session does NOT reveal

- The nature of magic. *(Could be. Could not be.)*
- The word "The Quiet" or any spoiler tokens.
- Whether Vega is a hostile or a helpful watcher.
- What {{pc:4}} actually found.
- Whether their names on the 1878 ledger are their souls, their bloodlines, a self-consistency accommodation, or something else. (Answer: it's *self-consistency accommodation* — the ledger's current shape (the shape the world now attests) only exists because the PCs' convergence at Bodie today required a self-consistent past to route through. The cheapest such past attests these names as having been on the ledger since 1878, and always having been there. There is one timeline; the previous shape does not persist anywhere; asking "was it different last week" is a category error from The Quiet's perspective. But do NOT say this at the table for at least four more sessions — the players are supposed to keep asking.)

## Difficulty knobs

If the table is **flagging** (low energy, hungry, tired):
- Skip most of Q2's search — Vega is present when they arrive at Building 7 and lets them in without a check ("I saw {{pc:4}} here two days ago, he said you might come, he asked me to hand you this key")
- Do the cast at Q3's beginning, not middle; use the remaining time on the argument

If the table is **hot** (leaning in, riffing, testing):
- Q2 exploration expands: they inventory the whole building, find a second ledger dated 1935 with different names but a similar handwriting to 1878
- Q3 adds a second uncanny thing that CAN'T be attributed to a single PC — a lantern relights itself when nobody's looking, a door unlatches, something small — foreshadowing that the town itself is doing this, not just the PCs
- Q4's voicemail can add "Bring your dice" or another explicit cliffhanger for Ep 4

If the session runs **long** (approaching 2 hours and Q4 hasn't started):
- Skip the Vega parting-card beat
- Compress Q4's voicemail — just play the last two sentences
- End on the ledger photograph in someone's phone

## Safety valves

- **If a player disengages** — {{pc:2}}'s caregiving instinct is a natural bridge; hand her a quiet in-fiction reason to check on the disengaged player's PC
- **If someone bulldozes** — see {{pc:4}}'s dramatic-irony note in the character sheets. {{pc:4}} isn't here; the bulldozer role should not accidentally be picked up by an absent character's shadow. Give {{pc:3}} (the observer) a chance to react physically and land the room.
- **Keep {{pc:3}}'s observer role live in {{pc:4}}'s absence.** During the set-piece opening, take the player of {{pc:3}} aside for 30 seconds: *"{{pc:4}} picked a geocaching hook because he thinks you'll do what he suggests. What do you notice about the fact that he picked YOUR hook, specifically?"* This keeps the {{pc:4}}↔{{pc:3}} dynamic warm even with one player absent, and lets the player of {{pc:3}} carry the private-knowledge beat forward.
- **If the accidental cast lands flat** — take one more swing at it later with a smaller, more specific effect. Do not force explanation. The goal is that the players *felt* something odd, not that they understood it.
