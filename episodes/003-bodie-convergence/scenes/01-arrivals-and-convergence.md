# Episode 3 — Set-Piece Opening
*The Bodie Convergence — DM reads / paraphrases; ~17 minutes total in practice.*

Play-order: **Recap** → **The months between** → **{{pc:4}}'s absence explained** → **Arrival vignettes** → **The convergence** → **hand off to Quest**.

---

> **⏱ Realistic total for this opening: ~17 min in practice.** The recap read-aloud is ~2.5 min (updated with Ep 2 detail); interim vignettes 30s each; {{pc:4}}'s absence 90s; four arrival vignettes 60-90s each; convergence 3-5 min real-time. ## Read-aloud recap (~2.5 min — longer than the original 90s because Ep 2 detail was added post-play)

> *"Months ago now, five strangers on a night flight to Taipei found themselves in each other's orbit. The plane had a fault. The part that fixed it turned up sealed and new inside a bag it had no business being in. You landed, you swapped numbers in the arrivals hall without quite saying why, and you kept a folded-up anti-static wrapper as the only souvenir."*
>
> *"You met again in San Francisco a few weeks later. It started at Ocean Beach in the afternoon, five of you standing on the sand trying to figure out what had actually happened up there. A group of teenagers with a bonfire tried to talk one of you into buying them beer. You did not. They stuck around anyway. When you finally pulled the anti-static bag out to look at it — really look at it — you noticed it carried a chain of custody stamped down the seam: manufacturer, maintenance contractor, United, SFO ground-support. And then the log just stopped. One of the teenagers said, 'so, like, whatever happened happened at the airport, right?' You added them to a group chat before you left. You still text with them sometimes."*
>
> *"You took a Waymo to SFO. {{pc:5}} asked the ticketing counter about 'a bag I found in my luggage' and got the phone number of the legal department. {{pc:4}} tried to file a lost-luggage claim for a bag he never checked and got nowhere. As you were leaving the counter, one of you spotted the gate agent from that Taipei flight — Reggie — walking into the back of the departure hall between boardings. He saw {{pc:4}}, and his face changed. He saw the rest of you and his face changed more. He said Yui's name, or heard it, and he almost sat down. He couldn't stop — he had a plane to push back — but he told you to meet him at eleven at an In-N-Out in Daly City, off the freeway. He'd be there. He needed to think."*
>
> *"He was. He ate a burger with five patties on it, which is a thing In-N-Out officially doesn't make, and told you, gently and completely without irony, that he did not remember holding anyone's bag at that gate. He asked if it was true Yui had almost been fired. When you said yes, he stopped eating for a while. You showed him the security footage one of you had already pulled off SFO's cameras. He watched himself do things he did not remember doing. Nobody at the table could explain it."*
>
> *"The next morning at a Starbucks at 19th and Irving there was a woman at the next table who was paying more attention to your conversation than she should have. You confronted her; she gave you a business card and left. A little online sleuthing turned up her address in the Outer Sunset, and something odder — the house has been owned by an unbroken chain of previous archivists going back decades. That afternoon, you drove to the address. She was expecting you. Bea Ferro. Insurance investigator, forty years of index cards, the previous keeper of the same job dead the same day she took it over. She showed you a card from 1987. She said, gently, that she wouldn't tell you what any of it meant. You left with a photocopy in a jacket pocket."*
>
> *"That was five months ago. It is now late winter."*
>
> *(pause)*
>
> *"Since then… well. Life."*

## The months between (~2 min, DM narrates per PC — do not read verbatim; adapt to voice)

> **⚠ DM-ONLY paragraph below. Do NOT read aloud. Do not paraphrase.**
> The italicized rationale is why belief cooled — it's a **cheapest-edit** effect (see world truths). If you read this at the table you leak the entire cosmology in one sentence. Skip straight to the per-PC vignettes below.

Each PC has quietly filed the strangeness away. The belief has *cooled*. *(In world terms: the cheapest self-consistent shape is one in which their belief cools — the substrate settles into the least-effort configuration for a witness's memory. Reality-editing runs cheapest-first regardless of direction; nobody is "cooling their belief," the shape just is what it is.)*

Speak to each present player, briefly, in the second person. Use ~30 seconds each:

**{{pc:1}} (Slot 1) —** *"You went back to your three-phone life. You did some contract work for a legal clinic that pays scale and doesn't ask questions. The Flipper Zero is in the same jacket pocket it always is. Twice you almost sent Bea Ferro a message, and twice you didn't. Your father's file is still open at SFPD Missing Persons."*

**{{pc:2}} (Slot 2) —** *"You worked. A lot. Night shifts at UCSF. Two migraines bad enough to send you home. You visit Ocean Beach at dawn most weeks. You told your best friend the whole plane story once, at brunch — she laughed and said 'that's such a you story,' and you found yourself laughing too. It's been a while since you thought about the anti-static bag."*

**{{pc:3}} (Slot 3) —** *"You filed six thousand death certificates and eleven thousand deed transfers. You sketched more notes for the thesis you keep meaning to write. You've almost applied to MA programs twice. You have not gone back to Bea Ferro's house. The note is still in the locket around your neck. You almost never touch it now — that's new."*

**{{pc:5}} (Slot 5) —** *"You're back at the warehouse. You carry the pistol legally, most days you don't think about it. ## Arrival vignettes (~60-90 sec each; play in table order or by whim)

The vignettes overlap in time but the PCs don't know they're all there yet. Play them one at a time. Keep each brief; the *convergence* is the payoff, not the arrivals.

**Do not worry about strict arrival order** — the vignettes reference each other's cars ({{pc:1}}'s SUV, {{pc:2}}'s Corolla, {{pc:5}}'s green pickup) because they arrive within about a fifteen-minute window; nobody watched anyone else park. If a player asks "wait, I saw a car of {{pc:1}}'s — was he already inside?", the answer is *"yes; the cars beat the people to the visitor center by a couple of minutes each."*

### {{pc:1}} arrives

*You drove up through the Owens Valley yesterday, six hours from the Bay, and got a motel room in Bridgeport. The dirt road into Bodie SHP is thirteen miles of washboard. You pull into the visitor lot at 9:14am. There are two other cars in the lot — a beat-up green pickup that looks like it has slept there, and a blue Corolla with a bike rack. The wind is the kind of cold that dries your eyes out immediately. There's snow on the ridges to the east. The visitor center is a repurposed shed with a hand-painted sign. The ranger inside is your age, maybe younger, and looks pleased to see anyone.*

*She hands you a photocopied map and a brochure. You skim the brochure while she talks. Halfway down, in nine-point type: **"Select outbuildings were requisitioned by federal agencies between 1942 and 1948 for classified purposes; records remain sealed."** You look up. She's mid-sentence about the mercury mining. You look back down. She's saying you'll want to see the Standard Mill first.*

*You do not see anyone you recognize.*

### {{pc:2}} arrives

*You caught a red-eye up to Reno, rented a car at 6am, and drove down. The bike-rack Corolla is you — you told the rental clerk you might bike a trail and forgot the bike existed by the time you left the Reno airport. There's a green pickup parked at the far end of the lot that looks like it's been slept in, and a beige rental SUV closer to the visitor center. You take the middle spot.*

*The visitor-center ranger tells you {{pc:4}}'s friend hasn't arrived yet — no one by that name has shown up. She says it warmly. You get the sense that in Bodie the ranger knows every visitor who's walked through the door today. There have been three, counting you.*

*The brochure has a line about federal agencies that catches your eye. You put the brochure in your back pocket. The wind is loud enough to be its own noise.*

### {{pc:3}} arrives

*You took the bus. Two buses, actually — Reno to Hawthorne, Hawthorne to Bridgeport, then a hitched ride the last thirteen miles from a fisherman heading to Bridgeport Reservoir who dropped you at the top of the Bodie road. You walked the last mile. It took you almost an hour and you weren't cold at any point during it. The town appears at the bottom of the road like something the desert is politely permitting.*

*The parking lot has three cars. You know somebody's here. You just don't know who yet. A green pickup, a Corolla with a bike rack, a beige SUV.*

*The visitor center ranger — young, friendly, brochure-armed — tells you the geocaching cluster is real and one of the caches is not where the GPS says it is. She wishes you luck.*

*Halfway to the ghost town proper, you stop. You have the specific feeling of a shape you know slightly, off to the left, in the group of buildings. You didn't see anyone. You just have the feeling. You resume walking. You take longer to reach the town than someone in a hurry would.*

### {{pc:5}} arrives

*You slept in your green pickup at the trailhead lot last night — it was that or spring for a Bridgeport motel and you didn't want to. You woke up cold at 5am and drove into Bodie proper at 8:30. Yours was the first car. The ranger opened up while you were sitting on the visitor-center steps drinking gas station coffee. She let you in early because you looked like you'd been up a while.*

*She talked. You mostly listened. She mentioned the federal-annex line in the brochure without seeming to think it was odd. You made a note to come back to it.*

*Now it's 9:30 and there are two other cars in the lot beside yours. None of them are {{pc:4}}'s. You're standing by the Standard Mill trying to work out what {{pc:4}} expected you to see when someone twenty feet away, backlit against the sun, tilts their head in a way you know.*

## The convergence (~3-5 min — play in real time; let it breathe)

Now the PCs see each other. Play out the recognition per pair. Suggested rhythm:

**First pair:** {{pc:5}} + {{pc:1}} (they know each other best; give them the warmest moment).

{{pc:5}} sees the tilt of {{pc:1}}'s head from twenty feet away and knows before he sees the face. They meet in the middle of the mill's shadow. {{pc:5}} says something like *"you did not."* {{pc:1}} says *"I figured you out about ninety seconds ago."*

**Second reveal:** {{pc:2}} walks up from the visitor center behind them. She sees them, stops, and — *before* she recognizes them from the plane — she recognizes the *shape* of them together. She has seen those two men in a bar mirror somewhere she can't place. Then it snaps.

**Third reveal:** {{pc:3}} has been watching for two minutes from the doorway of the assay office at the top of the hill. She could have joined them at any point. She comes down when she's ready.

**Let the players talk this out in-character.** Do not narrate over their reunion. If the table is stiff, prompt: *"{{pc:4}} set us up."* Someone will say it and the rest will follow.

Once the four of them are in one conversation, cut in:

> *"You all get a text at the same time. All four of your phones. The message is from {{pc:4}}'s number. There is no text — just an image. It's a photograph of the visitor-center brochure, one line highlighted in red pen. The line about federal agencies. Under the photograph, one word: 'Yes.'"*

*(Pause. Let the players react. Then:)*

> *"The signal on the four of your phones drops to zero in the same minute. The ranger walks past you toward the Standard Mill with a serious look on her face. She's on a radio. When she gets close enough for you to hear the radio, you hear a weather-service voice saying words like 'unexpected' and 'the entire eastern slope.'"*

**Hand off to the quest.** The storm is coming; the road out will be closed by afternoon; {{pc:4}} has told them where to look; they are stuck in Bodie together for the first time. Go to `ep3-quest.md`.

## DM notes for this opening

- **Read pace is slower than you think.** The recap read-aloud is 90 seconds; the arrivals should feel unhurried. If the table is chatting, let them; the vignettes still work if the players are half-listening.
- **The federal-annex brochure line is the seed.** Read it *exactly* verbatim to at least two of the four PCs. Do not embellish it. Do not repeat it in your own words the first time — let the players discover they've all seen it independently.
- **The four cars in the lot** are {{pc:1}}'s, {{pc:2}}'s (bike-rack Corolla), {{pc:3}}'s absence (she hitched), {{pc:5}}'s (the first car / truck). {{pc:4}}'s is not there. This is a *quiet* clue.
- **The ranger is not a NPC-of-note.** Give her a name (**Ranger Alaia Vega**) but resist making her important. She will get one small suspicious beat during the quest — see quest doc. Otherwise she is texture.
- **Silent-player firewall:** at no point in this opening does any character (NPC or PC) reference magic, The Quiet, casting, or the archivist by name in a way that would spoil. "That woman in the Sunset" is the maximum acceptable reference for Bea. Do not name Bea aloud until a player names her first.
- **Belief thermometer:** the PCs are starting today at "have rationalized." The brochure line + {{pc:4}}'s cryptic "yes" + the same-minute signal drop should nudge them one click warmer. The accidental cast in the quest will nudge them all the way back.
- **{{pc:4}}'s off-screen thread:** if a player asks the DM directly "where IS {{pc:4}}?" — the honest DM answer is *"we'll find out."* {{pc:4}}'s player is not here today; the character is doing whatever the DM will find useful to seed next session. Note the question so you can tie it to something specific later.
