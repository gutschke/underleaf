# Scene 03 — {{pc:4}}'s parallel track (optional)

**Optional scene.** Play this if {{pc:4}}'s player is at Ep 3's session; narrate its events during Scene 02 §voicemail if the player is absent. Either way, the events herein are canonical to the campaign timeline.

Ep 3's episode.json already flags this in the hooks list: *"If all five players are present, run Slot 4's convergence work in-fiction between sessions and open the episode with them delegating the on-site investigation to the others."* This scene is that playable material.

---

## When to run this scene

**All 5 players present (recommended):**
Run this scene FIRST, at the start of the Ep 3 session (~25-35 min). Then transition to Scene 01 (arrivals-and-convergence) with {{pc:4}} delegating: *"You four handle Bodie; I've got something else."* Scene 02 runs at Bodie as usual. Ep 3 ends with {{pc:4}}'s voicemail as canonically written (Scene 02 §voicemail), delivered here by the player instead of narrated.

**Note on {{pc:4}}'s player during Scene 02:** even with this alternate, {{pc:4}}'s PC is not physically at Bodie for the ~2h Scene 02 setpiece. Their player will effectively sit out that stretch. Making a player sit out for a session's centerpiece to build narrative tension is not unusual in this genre and can work well if the player is warned in advance and consents. If the DM wants to keep {{pc:4}}'s player table-engaged during Scene 02, options include: (a) quick intercut cutaways (2-3 min each) showing {{pc:4}}'s Sacramento observation/waiting between Bodie beats; (b) the player runs an NPC (Ranger Vega, the storm, or a "voice of Bodie" narrator) as a co-DM helper for that stretch; (c) split into two shorter sessions — Scene 03 in its own session before the Bodie session. All three add table complexity; the default (player sits out with consent) is often the cleanest choice.

**{{pc:4}}'s player absent:**
DO NOT run this scene at the table. Narrate its endpoint (Scene 02 §voicemail) as usual; the DM holds the full content herein as private context the player will inherit when they return. See §"Alternative: player absent" at the end.

**{{pc:4}}'s player present but arriving late (or leaving early):**
Skip this scene. {{pc:4}}'s PC arrives at Bodie with the rest of the party in Scene 01's opening; their off-page events get delivered as backstory in Ep 4.

- **Vivian Loewe** is alive and reachable — a 98yo daughter of the annex lead. She will drive Ep 4.
- The **8-ledger network** is a concrete count, not a hypothesis — {{pc:4}} extracts it from Eleanor Marsh's marginalia.
- **Eleanor Marsh** (Bea's 1987-decedent predecessor) is named as a link in the archivist chain.

## Scene structure — five beats, ~25-35 min if played

### Beat 1 — Bea revisit *(~5 min)*

{{pc:4}} goes to Bea Ferro's Outer Sunset house alone, about six weeks before Ep 3's Bodie trip. Bea recognizes them from Ep 2. She lets them look at her 1878 crew index card unsupervised.

**Read-aloud (arrival):**
> *"Bea opens the door and doesn't seem surprised to see you alone. 'I was wondering which of you would come first,' she says. 'You want to see the card.' She turns without waiting for an answer and walks back into the house. The cards are in the same battered wooden box in the same corner of the kitchen. She sets one on the table. 'This one, right? The Bodie crew.'"*

**The card** (per Ep 2 canon): a 5-person mining crew for the Standard Mill in 1878. Four names + initials match the modern surnames of {{pc:1}}, {{pc:2}}, {{pc:3}}, {{pc:4}}. The fifth entry has a coffee-stained illegible surname beneath the occupation *"physician."*

**What Bea will do:**
- Let {{pc:4}} photograph the card
- Refuse to explain: *"I write them; I don't read them. You'll understand or you won't."*
- Not stop {{pc:4}} from taking notes
- Not accompany them anywhere further

**What Bea will NOT do:**
- Explain what the name-match means
- Name the physician
- Point {{pc:4}} at any specific next step (the Sacramento lead is a discovery, see Beat 2)

**The margin lead.** As {{pc:4}} handles the card, they can flip it: on the back, in a hand not Bea's — older, ballpoint pen, dated 1979 — is a Sacramento self-storage unit address with the note *"paid up-front through 2035"* beside it. Bea does not comment on it. If asked, she says only: *"That's my predecessor's hand. She died in 1987. Whatever she was tracking is your business now, not mine."*

**{{pc:4}} leaves with:** the photograph of the card, notes about its contents, and the storage unit address.

### Beat 2 — Sacramento self-storage locker *(~10 min)*

{{pc:4}} drives 90 minutes to Sacramento and finds the unit. Standard commercial self-storage facility. The unit is paid up through 2035 by an LLC — **Argonaut Records Holdings** — that dissolved in 1998.

**Play options for entry:**

| Approach | Difficulty | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Social engineering (Influence) | DC 10 | Hit: admitted as "nephew tracing an estate." Partial: admitted, manager stays curious (may recur). Miss: refused; try again with a different pretext. |
| Paperwork pretext | DC 12 | Hit: admitted. Requires {{pc:4}} to have prepared documents matching the LLC name in advance. |
| Break-in | Off-archetype for Control-seeker | If pursued: mark of "acted against personal code"; DM decides consequences (facility cameras, silent alarm, etc.). |

**Inside the unit:**

- **Three bound ledgers** — spanning 1912–1934, 1948–1971, 1971–1995 — hand-annotated in the margins by a hand not Bea's
- **Loose annotated index cards** — similar to Bea's but in a different hand
- **Roughly a dozen sealed envelopes** labeled by year
- **Various photographs, papers**

**Search rolls (optional):** Insight DC 10 to piece together that the annotator is **Eleanor Marsh** (born 1922, died 1997), Bea's predecessor in the archivist chain. Free read for the ledger contents overview.

**Key discovery — a specific margin note in Marsh's hand, dated 1979:**

> *"Loewe's daughter still lives at [Sacramento suburban address]. She was 20 when the annex closed. She remembers everything he was too discreet to write down. She has been quietly waiting for someone to ask."*

This is {{pc:4}}'s Sacramento lead. It names *Loewe* — a name the party has never heard before, but which will resolve to Dr. Erik Loewe (wartime lead of Field Annex 7) once {{pc:4}} researches or asks.

**The eight-ledger count** also emerges from Marsh's marginalia: cross-references across ledgers Marsh cataloged from elsewhere establish that there are eight total in the network. Bea holds one. Marsh's three are now {{pc:4}}'s. Four more are held by other archivists Marsh named only obliquely.

**{{pc:4}} also finds photographs** that they may or may not choose to send to the group chat over the following weeks. If the DM wants to run the Ep 3 §7 photographs-drop beat (the brown-edged photograph of Bodie's clapboard row + four follow-up photos), the photographs are here:
- 1880-era Bodie residential row, brown at the edges, two figures with faces cropped
- Interior shot of a small clapboard room with shelving resembling Building 7 (annex era)
- Exterior shot of the Standard Mill in its working era
- A hand-written ledger page, over-exposed, mostly illegible
- A card from Bea's index set, matching one Bea showed the PCs

**{{pc:4}} leaves with:** the three ledgers, a subset of the loose material, the address, and their choice of photographs.

### Beat 3 — Drive + observation *(~5 min)*

{{pc:4}} drives to the address Marsh's marginalia points at. Small 1960s Sacramento ranch house, well-kept garden. In-character for a Control-seeker archetype, {{pc:4}} observes rather than knocking immediately. They spend a day or two watching from their car (or a nearby coffee shop; or a Motel 6 with a view of the street).

**What they see:**
- An elderly woman (~98) lives here with a professional caregiver (early 40s, protective)
- Regular routine: caregiver drives to the market Wednesdays and Fridays; woman stays home; no other visitors during the observation window
- No signs of surveillance, hostility, or unusual activity

**Optional rolls:**
- Insight DC 10 to notice the household routine
- Insight DC 12 to notice small tells about the caregiver's tenure (~3 years, hospice background; small details in wardrobe and body-language)

**No forced beats.** This is character-establishing rest between the storage-unit discovery and the confrontation of the first meeting.

### Beat 4 — First meeting *(~10 min)*

{{pc:4}} knocks. The caregiver — **Elena** — answers. {{pc:4}} introduces themself and asks to speak with the woman about her father. Elena is hesitant but ultimately admits them.

**The woman is Vivian Loewe.** 98 years old, daughter of **Dr. Erik Loewe** — wartime lead of Field Annex 7 at Bodie (1942–1948). She has kept her father's private papers for 65 years since his death in 1961.

**Read-aloud (when {{pc:4}} introduces themself):**
> *"Vivian sits very still for a moment. Then she says: 'You have his eyes. I don't mean that in the sentimental way. I mean the way he watched people. My father spent forty-five years watching people. It was his whole method. Sit down.'"*

**What Vivian tells {{pc:4}} at this first meeting:**
- Her father led a classified team at Bodie 1942–1948 under War Department cover
- She was 14–20 during his tenure; joined summer trips as a child and worked briefly as a young hire in the annex's last two years (1946–1948)
- The cover was strategic minerals; the real work was *"listening to how the world keeps its books"* (Loewe's phrase)
- Her father died in 1961 asking her to keep his work private
- She has kept the promise for 65 years
- She is dying now
- She wants to speak before she goes, but only to people she has judged trustworthy
- She judges {{pc:4}} to be *"one of the people I've been waiting for — but not the only one. There should be others."*
- She invites {{pc:4}} to return with the others — specifically, *"the ones whose names are on the ledger my father protected."* She names the four surnames matching {{pc:1}}, {{pc:2}}, {{pc:3}}, and {{pc:4}}.

**What Vivian does NOT tell {{pc:4}} at this first meeting:**
- The names of her father's specific correspondents (held for the group visit in Ep 4)
- The existence of her father's leather notebook (also held for Ep 4)
- Anything about pattern-observation mechanics
- Whether she has knowledge of the party's collective phenomenon (she does; withheld)

**{{pc:4}} leaves with:** a phone number, a promise to return with the others, and the sense that they have found something bigger than what they went looking for.

### About who {{pc:4}} was in this room — character note

**{{pc:4}} dropped the persona.** The party has spent months experiencing the Control-seeker archetype's carefully-cultivated public voice — the manipulator, the ambitious 21-year-old with a personal code, the person who volunteers a well-chosen fact only when it advances a plan. **Vivian did not get any of that.** With her, {{pc:4}} was quiet, careful, slower to speak, patient with her pauses, gentle when she needed to reach for something. They took the chair she pointed at without commentary. They asked one question at a time. They thanked her simply when she said something important. When it was time to go, they said so, said thank you, and left without lingering.

**The player should know this about their PC** — even though the persona is a real defense, {{pc:4}} has a softer register they can drop into when the room asks for it, and Vivian's room asked for it. Elena (the aide) saw all of this from the kitchen doorway and it is why she'll welcome {{pc:4}} warmly two days later when the party returns.

The DM does not need to voice this at the table if it's not being played out — but if {{pc:4}}'s player IS running this scene, invite them to try the register: *"This is not the same room as the group chat. What does {{pc:4}} sound like here?"*

### Beat 5 — The voicemail *(~2 min)*

On the drive home from Sacramento, {{pc:4}} tries to call the party. They are at Bodie by now — no cell signal. {{pc:4}} leaves the voicemail from Scene 02 §voicemail (delivered here by the player rather than narrated):

> *"The ledger you found: there are eight more like it. Bea has one. I have three. There are four more. You should figure out where they are. Also — the OTHER thing I've been chasing turned out to be bigger than I thought it was. I'll tell you when you're back."*

**{{pc:4}} deliberately does not name Vivian in the voicemail.** They want to bring the group to her in person, not describe her over the phone.
