# What Nia told you — five slips

---

## {{pc:1}}

> **In short:** you're good with machines — locks, phones, printers, systems. You can't tell a machine what to do next. You say what it *already did*, and it turns out that's what it did.

> **Cheap:** anything that could pass for luck — the lock that wasn't quite latched, the printer that didn't jam after all, the phone that had one bar. **Expensive, or impossible:** anything somebody already saw or heard go the other way — a person, a camera, a log. One witness makes it cost; a crowd can make it impossible. You can't aim it at people — only at the machine.

> **It isn't always easy to guess.** Say what you want, and ask the price before you commit.

**What it works on:** machines. A lock, a signal, a system, something that did the wrong thing.

**What sets it off:** the Flipper in your hand — never pointed at anything — and one sentence about what the machine *did*, in the past tense.

**What it costs:** pins and needles in that hand.

**Where it stops:** the machine, never the people around it. You change what it did, not what anyone thinks.

---

## {{pc:2}}

> **In short:** you help bodies. Someone is hurt, cold, choking or in pain, you want them okay — and they turn out a bit more okay than they should be. You don't need a plan: say out loud what you want for them — *"I just want her to breathe."*

> **Cheap:** a step a body could plausibly take — the bleeding slows, the pain eases a notch, the breath comes back. **Expensive, or impossible:** what a doctor would call a miracle, or what a room full of people just watched go the other way.

> **Sometimes you also feel** that something near you just got changed. That's noticing, not doing.

**What it works on:** bodies. Someone cold, choking, hurt, in pain — and you want them okay, badly. It works best when you don't stop to think.

**What it costs:** your sleep and your appetite, afterwards. That's the bill.

**The other half:** sometimes your body tells you that something near you was just changed. A hum, a pressure behind the eyes — a signal, not a price. It tells you *that* — never what, never who. You won't feel your own. You can try to listen for it on purpose, but if you felt nothing, there's usually nothing to find.

**Where it stops:** bodies. Not wallets.

**You're past the trying:** casting on purpose doesn't cost you extra any more, so backing doesn't change *your* price. Your reasons still count when you back someone else.

## {{pc:3}}

> **In short:** you notice what's missing. Look hard at a photo, a record, a list or a memory, and you see where a piece is gone — its shape, not what it was. Sometimes, if you want it badly, a piece comes back: a word steadies, a name surfaces.

> **Usually cheap:** seeing *that* something is gone, and its shape. **Harder:** bringing it back — the more people and records agree it was never there, the harder. **Never:** who did it.

**What it works on:** something missing or wrong. A record, a photo, a memory. You see where a piece is gone — its shape, where it was. Want it badly enough and sometimes what was there comes back: a name surfaces, a smudged word steadies.

**What sets it off:** looking hard, wanting to see what isn't there.

**What it costs:** a headache, when you push past what it'll give you. That one *is* the price.

**Where it stops:** never *who*. Pushing harder makes it blurry, not sharp.

---

## {{pc:4}}

> **In short:** you reach people. On your own, say someone's full name out loud and tap the pipe. If they can be reached, they pick up — or they're just there.

> **If a normal call would reach them, it's just a call.** Otherwise — **Cheap:** someone who's just busy. **Costlier:** someone avoiding you today. **Hard:** someone famous. Some people can't be reached at all, and it won't tell you why. It only opens the door: the talking is all you.

**What it works on:** one person you can't get hold of.

**What sets it off:** on your own, their full name, out loud, tapping the pipe. If they can be reached, they turn out to be reachable — they pick up, they're there.

**What it costs:** your voice, hoarse, for a while after.

**Where it stops:** it opens the door. Getting them to talk is just you. Some people can't be reached, and it won't tell you why — it all sounds the same. Nothing.

**Always alone.** Nobody can back this one.

**And don't go looking for someone's people unless they ask you to.**

---

## {{pc:5}}

> **In short:** you protect people by standing in the way. Step between someone and whatever's coming at them — a dog, a bike, a gull, a car — and it goes somewhere else. The gun stays holstered.

> **Cheap:** when there's an easy somewhere-else — the dog swerves after a pigeon, the bike clips a bin. **Expensive, or impossible:** nowhere for it to go, or a crowd watching. It never lands on another person.

**What it works on:** something coming at someone.

**What sets it off:** you step in between. Holstered. The moment you draw, you're trying, and trying costs.

**What it costs:** a bad night's sleep, and your neck the next day.

**Where it stops:** whatever's coming goes somewhere else — onto a thing, never onto a person. It works on anything with a direction: a person, an animal, a car. Not paperwork.

---

## For all five

> **How to use it:** (1) say what you want, in plain words; (2) the DM tells you the price; (3) go ahead, or don't. Free and Cheap just happen. Costly and Hard need a roll, and cost stress whatever you roll.

> **Rule of thumb for all five:** the more it looks like ordinary luck, the cheaper. The more people, cameras or records would have to be wrong, the more it costs — up to impossible. (For {{pc:4}}, it's how hard the person is to reach.)

**Trying on purpose costs more** — unless two of the others back you, each saying what *they* get out of it. Three reasons, not three voices. "Me too" isn't a reason.
