## What this session is

**A picnic by a model-boat lake, where somebody finally tells them.** Nia Chen — the name on the index card that fell out of Bea's door — has been putting them off for six weeks. Today she shows up with a canvas tote, a bag of snacks and a battery printer, and **says it plainly: all five of you can do this.**

**Then she shows them, and they show her.** A printer jams; {{pc:1}} says one sentence and five pages turn out to have printed. The pages are the rules. Over snacks, Nia gets them to tell her the strangest things that have happened since the plane, and gives each one of three answers: *that was you*, *that was just you*, or *nobody knows*. **By the end of the game, every one of them has been told what their magic is: what kind of problem it works on, what sets it off, what it costs, and where it stops.**

**Then she leaves, and they do one on their own.** {{pc:4}} reaches her when she forgets her bag. The five pick a stranger with a small problem and help, together, and it works. **The season ends on them asking what the next thing would cost.**

**Nobody fails tonight.** Nothing in this session is set up to go wrong. **Stress is zero unless a player pushes for something big.** It ends on a high.

**One thing does not come off, and it is nobody's fault:** Nia's friend was going to come, and doesn't.

## What you need to know before you start

**Nia Chen.** Early thirties, Taiwanese-American, born in San Jose, lives in a small Mission apartment. Short hair she clearly cut herself. Worn Blundstones, a canvas tote she's had since college, a bike. Freelance UX researcher — **she carries a little battery printer for consent forms**, and it jams constantly. **Voice:** casual, quick to laugh, interrupts herself, uses your first name on the second sentence. **An older cousin, not a teacher.** She has done what they are starting and is glad they made it this far. **She knows the craft** — how it feels, what it costs, that pushing makes it refuse, that aiming costs more, that casting with people you know works. **She does not know** why the flight happened, what the ledgers are for, or what any of it means, and she says so without embarrassment.

**How they reached her.** In mid-February an index card fell out of Bea's door with Nia's name and number on it. {{pc:3}} left a voicemail; Nia texted back and said yes to lunch, then named no day for a month. At the end of Mei-Mei's party she finally did — to {{pc:3}}'s phone: *"saturday the 27th, lunch, noon-ish? I'll send you where that morning. bring whoever."* A minute later: *"I was going to bring a friend but she hasn't really wanted to see anybody lately. we'll see."*

**Bea Ferro**, the archivist from the Outer Sunset, has not been seen since early February. **She is fine, and nothing about her happens tonight** unless {{pc:4}} reaches for her (Scene 5).

**What the players already believe.** After seven sessions they have concluded that pushing doesn't work: *"it happens when it happens."* Several of them already suspect they're doing something. Rosa, the old square dancer in Berkeley, half-told them she had met people who could do what they were learning to do. **So tonight is confirmation, not news.** Say it bluntly.

**Spreckels Lake.** On the western side of Golden Gate Park, at 36th Avenue and Fulton. A rectangular lake built for model boats; the **San Francisco Model Yacht Club** keeps a clubhouse at one end and races radio-controlled sailboats on Saturday afternoons. Benches along the bank, ducks, geese, gulls that have learned what food looks like, a concrete turtle that real turtles sun themselves on, and **the bison paddock just across the road**. It's late March: **warm and clear.** **No security cameras** — Nia checked; it's why she picked it.

**Quickly.** The bubble-tea and snack shop on Irving Street (at 22nd Avenue). The players love it. On the way, they pick up snacks (Scene 0).

**The yacht-club man.** Sixties, windbreaker, rigging a radio-controlled sailboat at the next bench, half-listening. **No name.** He matters twice: he says *"nothing came out"* at the printer, and he is the one who finds the pages.

**The friend.** Nia's friend, who was going to come. **She is never named tonight.** If anyone asks: *"Not mine to give. Give her a week."*

**{{pc:4}}'s uncle**, who engraved his pipe, is **alive** (it matters if {{pc:4}} reaches for him).

## Scene 1 — Nia, and the empty spot on the bench · 12 minutes · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

### What happens (Scene 1)

**Nia is on the far bench, away from the clubhouse,** a bike locked to the bin, a canvas tote at her feet, a paper bag of snacks of her own. She sees them, stands, waves with both arms, laughs at herself for it. **First names on the second sentence.** She hugs nobody; she fist-bumps whoever offers.

**There is room on the bench for seven.** She has kept a space at the end with her jacket on it. **Don't point at it.**

### She says it, early · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**Within the first two minutes, before anybody opens a snack, she says the plain thing.** This is not news — Rosa half-said it in Berkeley — so it is said as *confirmation*, flat and warm:

> **NIA:** *"Okay. Before the food gets cold. All five of you can do this. Not 'might'. You already have. You just didn't have a word for it."*

**Let it sit.** If anyone asks "do what?", she laughs:

> **NIA:** *"You know what. The things that kept happening. I'll show you in a sec, the printer's being dramatic."*

### The one rule she gives them up front · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**Before her phone buzzes** — it matters that this comes first:

> **NIA:** *"One thing, before I forget. Don't try to meet more than two of us at a time. It stops working when there are too many of us in a room. I don't know why. It just does."*

**Someone will say "but there's five of us."** Have it ready:

> **NIA:** *"You five seem to be fine. I don't know why. Time, maybe."*

**And move on.** Do not let her elaborate.

### The friend cancels · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**A minute later, Nia's phone buzzes.** She reads it, and her face does one small thing.

> **NIA:** *"She's not coming. That's okay."*

**She picks her jacket up off the end of the bench and puts it on.** That is the whole beat. **Never connect it to the rule she just gave them** — her friend hasn't wanted to see anybody lately, and that is the reason. If a player asks who:

> **NIA:** *"A friend. Not mine to give. Give her a week."*

### If they brought somebody · **only if it happens**

**Nia's text said "bring whoever."**

- **If they brought Alaia** (the ranger from Bodie, {{pc:2}}'s contact — she can do this too): Nia likes her at once, and they'll swap numbers. Then Alaia's phone goes, and she has to leave — *"Park thing. Of course."* — **before the printer comes out.**
- **Anyone else:** they're welcome. When Nia gets the printer out, she cheerfully sends them to watch the boats: *"Go see who wins. I'm boring them with paperwork."*

### {{pc:4}} and Nia

**If {{pc:4}} performs** — the charm, the pitch, the schemes — **Nia laughs at the bit, and answers the plain version better.** She is warm to him. **She is not the third adult in a row who is unimpressed by him.** If he says something plainly, give him a real answer, quickly.

## Scene 2 — The printer · 15 minutes · **never cut** · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

### What you need to know (Scene 2)

**This scene teaches the one rule that matters most for the rest of the campaign:** casting on purpose costs more, **unless two of the others back you, each saying what *they* get out of it.** **Three reasons, not three voices.** The players see one thing change — the price you say out loud — and then they see the cast happen.

**You will say the price before anything happens.** That is the whole lesson. Say it plainly, as a number they can hear move.

### What happens (Scene 2)

**Nia pulls a small grey printer out of the tote** and sets it on the bench between the snacks.

> **NIA:** *"I printed you the boring parts. Well. I'm about to."*

She taps her phone. **Six copies** — *"six, habit."* The printer whirs, pulls in a sheet, crunches, and stops. **An amber light blinks.** One page sticks half out of its mouth, chewed and inked.

**Put the crumpled sheet on the table.** **Show the first phone card, and read it:**

> *"Nia's phone says: Paper jam. 0 of 6 printed."*

**The yacht-club man at the next bench, without looking up from his rigging:**

> **YACHT MAN:** *"Nothing came out. It ate the first one."*

**He must say this before anyone casts.** It is what makes the price what it is.

> **NIA:** *"It does this. It's been weird all week."*

### {{pc:1}} · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**{{pc:1}} has the Flipper on him; he always does.** Wait a few seconds to see whether he moves. Then, one rung at a time:

> **DM:** *"{{pc:1}}, it's a machine that just did something wrong."*

> **DM:** *(after thirty seconds)* *"You've got the Flipper. Say what it did. Past tense."*

> **DM:** *(last rung)* *"Try: 'It printed five before it jammed.'"*

**If the player still freezes, ask out of character whether they want to, and if so, narrate the line for him.** The handouts come out of this scene one way or another.

### The price — say it out loud, before anything happens

**The moment {{pc:1}} commits:**

> **DM:** *"{{pc:1}}, on your own, trying on purpose: that's Costly. A roll, and a stress."*

**If a player asks why it's so expensive when he has the Flipper:**

> **DM:** *"It's a big change. That man said out loud that nothing came out. And you're trying on purpose, on your own."*

**Wait.** Somebody will offer to help.

- **If they just echo him** — *"yeah, make it print"* — **the price stays where it is:**

  > **DM:** *"Still Costly."*

  **Then ask each of them, once:**

  > **DM:** *"What do you get out of this?"*

  **Take whatever they say. Do not judge it.** There is no right answer. A reason only has to be theirs.

- **When two of them have each said their own reason**, say it like a fact:

  > **DM:** *"Cheap. No roll."*

- **If nobody echoes him naturally**, Nia does it for them, laughing:

  > **NIA:** *"If you'd all just said 'make it print', it'd still be Costly. What do you two actually want out of it?"*

- **If nobody backs him at all**, Nia teaches — this is the one place she does:

  > **NIA:** *"Back him. Say what you want out of it."*

- **If he still rolls alone**, it is a normal Costly roll, and 1 stress whatever happens:
  - **10 or more:** it works, as below.
  - **7–9:** it works, but only four pages: *"Got four out before it choked."* Nia prints the fifth later. **Still give out all five handouts** — she has a spare in the tote.
  - **6 or less:** nothing changes. A minute later the jam clears by itself and the printer prints normally. Nia: *"Sometimes it's just the printer."* The handouts arrive anyway. Then Nia, gently: *"Next time, let them back you."*

**{{pc:2}} has already had her release.** A *release* is a moment, earlier in the campaign, when a character stopped trying and it happened anyway; after it, casting on purpose no longer costs her extra. {{pc:2}}'s was the small talk with Yui after the grape. **So backing doesn't change the price of *her* casts — but she can back someone else, and her reason counts.**

### What changes

**Nothing changes that anybody sees.** The light still blinks amber. Then the yacht-club man puts his boat down, leans over — *"Here, I'll get that out for you"* — pulls the chewed sheet free, and there are **five clean pages** in the tray underneath.

> **YACHT MAN:** *"Huh. Got five out before it choked."*

**He has just contradicted himself, and he does not notice.** He goes back to his boat.

**Swap to the second phone card, and read it:**

> *"Nia's phone now says: 5 of 6 printed. Paper jam on page 6."*

> **DM:** *"{{pc:1}}, pins and needles in your Flipper hand. It fades."*

**Nia, quietly delighted:**

> **NIA:** *"You said it, and it did. That was you."*

**A beat. Then, as an afterthought, not as proof:**

> **NIA:** *"Funny. I remember both."*

### {{pc:1}} is told what his magic is · 90 seconds

> **NIA:** *"Machines. Locks, signals, systems. Something does the wrong thing, you hold that — "* (the Flipper) *" — never point it, and you say what it did. Past tense. And it turns out that's what it did. Pins and needles after, yeah? That's the bill. And it's the machine, never the people. You can't make that guy think anything. You just changed what the printer did."*

**Do not say that {{pc:1}}'s magic "never finds anything out."** If anyone brings up how he knew {{pc:4}}'s shoe size back in Bodie, see the companion sheet, entry 3.

### The pages

**Hand {{pc:1}}'s player the five handouts.** He deals them out. **Do not read it aloud.** Nia taps one line on the top copy — the boxed rule about backing:

> **NIA:** *"That bit. Three reasons, not three voices. You just did it."*

**The chewed page stays on the table, where everyone can see it, until the end of the session.** Never say whose copy it was.

## Scene 3 — "That was you" · 30 minutes · **never cut the gull or the five namings**

### What you need to know (Scene 3)

**This is the talk, but it is never announced as a game.** Nia asks them for the strangest things that have happened since the plane and gives one of three answers each time. **The repetition makes it a game.** Because it is never announced, you can stop it at any moment and nobody feels a game was cut off.

**Every past moment they are likely to raise is on the companion sheet, `past-moments.md`,** numbered, with what actually happened, the answer, and Nia's line. **Keep it open.** If someone raises something, find it, and say *"I'm on 14"* to yourself.

**The four answers:**

- **"That was you."** Their magic. **The first time a player hears it, Nia goes on to tell them what their magic is** — what kind of problem it works on, what sets it off, what it costs, and where it stops. About ninety seconds; **{{pc:2}}'s and {{pc:4}}'s take closer to three minutes.**
- **"That was just you."** Skill, nerve, charm or luck. **Always a compliment** — often the better answer.
- **"Nobody knows."** Something strange happened, and nobody can tell who did it. **Only for the moments listed as "nobody knows" on the sheet.**
- **"Not yours."** For something no one at the table did — Bea's card in the door, Nia's own text. One ordinary line: *"Oh, that was Bea."*

**And one more, for when you know the answer and Nia doesn't:** she says **"I don't know."** Never "nobody knows" about something you know.

**For anything not on the sheet:** if one of them *did* it, *"that was just you."* If it *happened to* them, *"not yours,"* in one line. **Never invent a new "that was you" or a new "nobody knows" at the table.**

**One big mystery per game.** The flight, the five strangers on it, the 1878 names at Bodie. The first time one comes up:

> **NIA:** *"That's the big one. Nobody knows."*

**Then go straight to the next player.** That is what keeps this from turning into a history lesson.

### Nia opens it

> **NIA:** *"Okay. Tell me the weirdest thing that's happened to any of you since that plane. I've got three answers, and you'll hear them a lot. 'That was you.' 'That was just you' — which is the better one, honestly. And 'Nobody knows.'"*

**Go in this order: {{pc:1}}, {{pc:2}}, {{pc:3}}, {{pc:5}}, {{pc:4}}.** Ask each by name. **Anyone can offer a moment about someone else** — that makes it a conversation, and it rescues a quiet player.

### {{pc:1}} · 1 minute

**He's already had his: the printer, ten minutes ago.**

> **NIA:** *"You I've seen. Next."*

**If he offers something else,** look it up on the sheet (entries 1–6).

### {{pc:2}} · 4 minutes · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**Ask her by name.** If she hesitates:

> **DM:** *"{{pc:2}} — the fire at Bodie, or the grape?"*

**Most likely she offers the fire in the Union Hall at Bodie** (sheet entry 8): trapped by the blizzard, she tore up her own satchel for kindling, cried *"I just want everyone to survive"*, and then the bag was whole, and there was a fire, and wood, and matches.

> **NIA:** *"Oh, that was you. Completely."*

**Then tell her what her magic is** — both halves. The lines are at the top of {{pc:2}}'s section on the sheet. **The second half is new to everyone:** her headaches. **Point it at today:**

> **NIA:** *"And the other half. You felt something when the printer went, didn't you?"*

**Lean over and ask her player quietly: "Did you feel anything?"**
- **If she says yes, or isn't sure:** it was a low hum behind the eyes, gone in a second. (If nobody backed {{pc:1}} and the cast was Costly, it was a throb, not a hum.) Tell her so.
- **If she says no:** use the drive to Vivian's instead (sheet entry 12): the migraine that flared near Vacaville, and the way it let go when she stepped onto Vivian's front walk.

> **NIA:** *"That was you noticing. Not doing. It tells you that something near you just got changed. Never what. Never who. And you won't feel your own."*

**Where hers stops:** if anyone remembers her wishing for a thousand dollars in Bodie (sheet entry 13):

> **NIA:** *"That's where yours stops. Bodies. Not wallets."*

### {{pc:3}} · 4 minutes · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**Ask her by name.** If she says nothing:

> **DM:** *"{{pc:3}} — the outlines at Bodie?"*

**Last rung: Nia asks the others.**

> **NIA:** *"Anyone seen {{pc:3}} stare at something that isn't there?"*

**Somebody will remember the outlines at Bodie** (sheet entry 15). If nobody does, tell it in one sentence from the sheet, and Nia rules.

**Tell her what her magic is** (the lines are at the top of her section on the sheet): **something missing, or a record that's wrong.** She sees the outline of what used to be there. **Her headaches are the price** — *"that's what pushing costs."*

**What you need to know:** {{pc:3}} had a brother, Kai, whom nobody else remembers — not her parents, not any record. She wears a locket with a note in his handwriting inside. **The party has not been told about the note.**

**If Kai comes up in any form** — or she touches the locket — **this is private:**

> **NIA:** *"That one's yours. Tell me later, just us."*

**Then hand her a "Later. Just us." slip** and move on. **"Later" does not come tonight.** **Never say "I can see the locket"** or anything that suggests Nia knows about the note inside it.

### {{pc:5}} · 5 minutes · **HAS TO HAPPEN** · the high point

**Ask him for a moment.** He will probably offer the piñata at Mei-Mei's party, where the smallest kids kept leaning on him instead of hitting it (sheet entry 23).

> **NIA:** *"That was just you. Kids know."*

**Then, straight away, the gull.** {{pc:2}} is holding something — an egg puff waffle if she bought one. **A gull lands on the back of the bench, looks at her hand, and drops, wings out, straight at it.**

> **DM:** *"{{pc:5}}, you're right next to her."*

> **DM:** *(after thirty seconds, or if nobody moves)* *"It's coming at {{pc:2}}'s hand. Half a second. What do you do?"*

**If he steps between them** — physically, holstered — **it veers off and takes a waffle box from the empty end of the bench.** Never a person, never the chewed page.

> **NIA:** *"That was you."*

**Then tell him what his magic is:**

> **NIA:** *"Something comes at someone behind you, you step in, and it goes somewhere else. Onto a thing, never a person. Holstered. The second you draw, you're trying, and trying costs. It'll cost you sleep tonight. And that neck. And it doesn't work on paperwork."*

> **DM:** *"{{pc:5}}, your neck flares, sharp, then settles."*

**If he draws the pistol in his description,** stop and check out of character: *"At a picnic, with kids around? Really?"* **Nobody draws a gun in this campaign.**

**If he doesn't step in:** the gull gets the waffle. {{pc:2}} is fine; everyone laughs. **Then prompt the car from the second session** (sheet entry 22):

> **DM:** *"{{pc:5}} — the night you left Bea's, the first time. The car that was behind you for three turns. What happened to it?"*

> **NIA:** *"That was you. Your first one, I'd bet."*

**Then the naming above, and add:** *"Next one that comes at her, stand in front of her."* (Scene 6 has a goose, if he wants a second chance.)
