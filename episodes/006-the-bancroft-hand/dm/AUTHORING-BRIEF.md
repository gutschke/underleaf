## The three things Ep 6 must not get wrong

1. **Rosa's ledger contains no reference to any other keeper. Ever. Across forty
   years.** This is B1's deterministic floor — the recognition rendered as an
   artifact, so it fires whether or not a player says the line aloud. **It is the
   session's load-bearing object.**
2. **{{pc:2}}'s realization lands privately, in the garden**, and Rosa registers
   it and says nothing — but *visibly*. A destabilizer nobody witnesses is good
   authorship and zero table effect.
3. **Rosa does not name the network.** That line is Nia's, at the Act I→II hinge,
   and it lands once.

## Proposal A — the outline as written *(recommended baseline)*

**Bancroft reading room → Rosa's terraced garden.** The archive *fails*: the
finding aid points at a person in informal custody rather than a shelf, which
inverts the records-hunt pattern instead of repeating it. Rosa's ledger has been
on her kitchen table the whole time and she offers it at the end, because it was
never the point.

**Fires:** A5 begins ({{pc:1}}) · A3 ({{pc:2}}'s realization) · B13 · B1 partial ·
U9 first letter · T7 fires or retires.

**Strengths:** every arc-node is already placed, the register is a genuine third
(elder, domestic, unhurried) after Park Station's kinetics and Strada's peer
noise, and the archive-fails inversion is the best single idea in the outline.

**Risks:** the outline calls it **HEAVY** and it now has to also carry the
un-performed-{{pc:4}} room. **Something has to give.** Candidate cut: fold the
Bancroft desk into a single scene rather than two locations.

---

## Proposal B — invert the day: Rosa first, Bancroft second

Same content, reversed. **8:20 at the house**, then Rosa sends them down to
Bancroft in the afternoon with a call slip in her own handwriting — and *that* is
what fails.

**Why it might be better:** the failure lands after they have met the person it
is about, so it reads as *the institution cannot hold her* rather than as another
dead end. It also puts the garden early, when the table is fresh, which is where
{{pc:2}}'s realization wants to be. **8:20am sharp is a better cold open than a
reading room.**

**Cost:** loses the archive-then-person escalation the outline was built on.

---

## Proposal C — one location, all day *(the tightening option)*

**Cut Bancroft entirely this session.** The whole episode is Rosa's house and
garden, 8:20 to early afternoon. The finding-aid failure happens off-screen — she
already tried, years ago, and tells them so.

**Why:** the session budget has been tight five times running, and the standing
preference is to tighten rather than extend. One location, one register, one
elder, and every PC gets a room in it: the garden, the kitchen, the call slips in
a shoebox, the slope, the terraces.

**This is also the cleanest home for the un-performed {{pc:4}}** — a whole day at
an elder's house is the exact shape that produced his persona-drop with Vivian and
Bea.

**Cost:** {{pc:5}} loses the call-slip desk, which was his only written beat. He
needs a new one — the terraces need work and he is the one who does work.

## Out-of-the-box options

*Optimising for: engaged, on their feet, a little surprised · episode-shape
variety · few battles available · puzzles, escapes, interpersonal conflict, minor
in-universe stakes.*

### D. The eviction morning — **a clock, not a search**

Rosa is fine. **Her neighbour is not.** They arrive at 8:15 into a small,
ordinary crisis: a 40-year tenant three doors down is being moved out today, and
Rosa has decided the party is going to help. Boxes, a landlord's agent, a
sheriff's notice, four hours.

**The ledger conversation happens between carried boxes**, which is the most
Rosa-shaped delivery imaginable and means the session's load-bearing artifact
arrives while everyone's hands are full.

**Why it is good:** a hard clock, real stakes that are entirely mundane, and
{{pc:5}} the tenants'-rights organiser walks into the one scene in the campaign
built for him. **{{pc:4}}'s mayoral campaign meets an actual constituent.**
Puzzle-shaped (what can be saved in four hours) without being a records hunt.

**Risk:** it could eat {{pc:2}}'s realization. Protect the garden beat by putting
it *after* the crisis resolves, when the house is quiet.

### E. The Saturday she forgot — **{{pc:4}}'s room, made structural**

They arrive at 8:15. **Rosa has no idea who they are.** Not sinister, not
cosmological: she is 79 and she wrote it in a paper calendar under the wrong
week.

**The session becomes an hour of being politely not-let-in** — and the only thing
that works is the register that cannot be performed at. **The party has to earn a
morning they thought they had booked**, and {{pc:4}}'s usual approach is exactly
wrong in a way he can feel.

**Why it is good:** it is the un-performed-{{pc:4}} prerequisite turned into the
episode's spine rather than a beat bolted on. Interpersonal, no combat, genuinely
surprising, and the recovery is the content.

**Risk:** it is a whole session spent on one PC's arc. **Only run this if the
persona list comes back rich** — otherwise it is a session about a problem the
player has not agreed is a problem.

### F. Two rooms at once — **a split party, on purpose**

Rosa can only take two of them at a time; the garden path is narrow and she is
old and she says so. **The other three go to Bancroft alone.**

Intercut. The reading room half hits the finding-aid failure with no elder to
soften it; the garden half gets Rosa undivided. **Neither half knows what the
other learned until they compare notes in the car**, and the two accounts do not
quite line up — not cosmologically, just because people hear different things.

**Why it is good:** it is a shape this campaign has never used, it doubles the
per-PC surface in a HEAVY session, and the compare-notes scene is free drama.

**Risk:** intercutting is the hardest thing to run tired, and this table has run
long five times. **Do not choose this one without a clock card that survives it.**

## Recommendation

**Proposal C or D, with B's reversal folded in.** Both cut the location count,
both give the un-performed register a real home, and D gives {{pc:5}} and
{{pc:4}} rooms the outline currently owes them. **A is the safe baseline if the
break has been long and the appetite is for executing rather than redesigning.**

**Hold F.** It is a good idea for a session where the table is fresh and the
clock has slack — possibly Ep 9 or Ep 12.

## Does the outline still hold?

**Mostly yes. Three adjustments to consider:**

1. **Ep 7 is still overloaded.** It carries A9 (the campaign's first
   moment-of-release), R({{pc:3}}) beginning, {{pc:5}}'s deferred A7, plus T1, T2,
   T4, A8, B15 and Nia's date. It is labelled MEDIUM and it is not. **A9 is the
   gate-opener for every subsequent release** — if anything slips out of Ep 6, it
   must not land here.
2. **{{pc:4}} has no arc-node in Eps 6–9.** The outline says so explicitly, and
   Ep 5 has now made that worse rather than better: he had the best moment of the
   session and there is nowhere for it to lead. **His realization gate is also
   undefined and open** — see `dakota-phase-gates.md`. **This is the outline's
   largest structural gap and it has been flagged twice.**
3. **Ep 8's arrival of Nia is six in-game weeks after she agrees to lunch.** That
   gap is now deliberate and explained, but it means Eps 6 and 7 must carry the
   party's impatience visibly rather than ignoring it.
