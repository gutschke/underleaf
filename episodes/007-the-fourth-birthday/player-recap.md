# The story so far

*Read aloud at the start of the session. About three and a half minutes.*

Last summer, five strangers took the same night flight from San Francisco to Taipei. Halfway across the Pacific the plane had a cracked connector and no spare on board. Then a brand-new one — sealed, no paperwork, no maker's trail — turned up in {{pc:4}}'s carry-on. The plane made Taipei. {{pc:4}} kept the empty bag it came in.

Weeks later, at a Daly City In-N-Out at eleven at night, the gate agent Reggie ate a five-patty burger and watched the airport footage with you: a flight attendant, Yui, holding {{pc:4}}'s bag at the gate, which crew must never do. Reggie remembered none of it. Yui nearly lost her job over it.

The next morning, the woman at the next table in a Starbucks turned out to be Bea Ferro, who keeps forty years of index cards about things like this.

In February, {{pc:4}} sent the other four to Bodie, the ghost town, on four different excuses, and didn't come himself. A storm shut you in overnight. You found an 1878 crew roster with four of your surnames on it, and a fifth too water-damaged to read. Then his voicemail: there are eight ledgers, and he had known more than he'd said.

At his apartment he showed you three of them. Then you drove to Sacramento, in two cars. Vivian Loewe, ninety-eight, gave {{pc:4}} her father's notebook, with pages he had cut out himself, and sent you to Rosa Vega in Berkeley.

At the police station, Detective Ortiz showed {{pc:1}} one page: in 2018 somebody paid to search a dead company's records, and {{pc:3}} saw that they had been looking for a person. The name that came back was {{pc:1}}'s father's. Ortiz had written {{pc:4}} off — until he beat everyone to what the dates meant, and she changed her mind out loud. Nobody has disproved his theory that every archivist worked for the same insurance company. A clerk fixed a filing error, and Ortiz now thinks the whole thing is probably nothing.

In Berkeley, Alaia, the ranger from Bodie, bought the whole round because {{pc:2}}'s work impressed her, and gave you her mother's number. Bea is missing. An index card fell out of her door: Nia Chen, and a phone number. Nia said yes to lunch, and hasn't said when.

Then you went square dancing with Rosa. Six people, one car, four blocks; {{pc:2}} raised the seatbelts and was told *"It's four blocks, dear."* A dancer drafted {{pc:1}} on the strength of his jacket, and ladies well past eighty took turns at {{pc:5}}. Back in her kitchen, Rosa let {{pc:3}} photograph every page of the ledger she has kept for forty-eight years, and would not let it leave the house: it belongs under the microwave. She told you she had stopped something she used to do, years ago, for somebody else's sake, and would not say what. And she has never met anyone else who keeps a book like hers.

And this week, Reggie texted.
