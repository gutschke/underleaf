## What this session is

**A four-year-old's birthday party in a park by the Bay, and nothing to investigate.** Yui Tanaka — the flight attendant who held {{pc:4}}'s bag at the SFO gate on the night of the flight, and nearly lost her job over it — wants to meet the people who never blamed her. Her daughter turns four. There is too much food.

**It is the only session in the whole campaign where all five of them are together with nothing to find.** Let it be that. Let scenes run a few minutes long. Five people wanting different things from one afternoon, none of them in each other's way, is the texture — write no beat for it and do not fill it with plot.

**Four things happen to four of them, and each is small.** {{pc:1}} fixes something nobody else will. {{pc:5}} notices that the smallest guests keep choosing him. {{pc:3}} reads a photograph somebody would rather she didn't. {{pc:4}} gets a room with nobody in it to impress.

**And then, for ninety seconds, a child stops breathing, and {{pc:2}} is there before she has decided to be.** It resolves. Afterwards, in the quiet, something small happens for her that she was not trying to do.

**The emergency is real and weightless at once.** Nobody is harmed. Nobody is to blame. Nothing is behind it. It is a grape.

## What you need to know before you start

**Yui Tanaka.** Mid-thirties, flight attendant on the San Francisco–Taipei route for six years. Hair in a tight bun, tired around the eyes. **Half-Japanese (her father), half-Chinese-Filipino (her mother)**; born in Daly City, grew up in Cupertino, lives in Millbrae, near the airport. She chose Mandarin for herself as a kid and speaks it well; it is why the airline keeps her on the Taipei route. **Efficient, professional, a little clipped when tired. When stressed she adjusts her cuff with her thumb.**

**What happened to her.** On the night of the flight, at the gate, she held {{pc:4}}'s bag for a few seconds — something flight attendants must never do. The gate camera recorded it. **She does not remember it.** The airline wrote her up; it cost her the purser job she had been applying for. **She did not lie and she is not confused: she simply has no memory of it**, and nobody has ever believed that except, apparently, five passengers who went asking. **The party has never met her.** They know her name, and they have seen her on the footage.

**Mei — "Mei-Mei".** Her daughter, **four today**. Legally **Mei**, a Japanese name from Yui's father's side. **Everybody calls her Mei-Mei.** Yui's mother started it — the Filipino habit of doubling a name as an endearment — and then the Taipei crews heard *mèimei*, "little sister", which is what Taiwanese adults call any small girl, and took it up. **Several players speak Mandarin. Drop this cleanly or not at all** (see Scene 1 and Scene 3). She was sick most of the winter. She is fine now and extremely four.

**Inez.** Yui's wife, a graphic designer. **Background only: she holds Mei, she cuts cake, she has no lines.** If anybody asks her name, it is Inez.

**Reggie Okeke.** The gate agent from the night of the flight, late twenties, black-rimmed glasses, calls people *boss*. The party met him once, weeks after the flight, at a Daly City In-N-Out at 11pm, where he ate a burger with five patties and watched himself on security footage doing something he does not remember doing. **He is Yui's friend and he is the one who sends the invitation.** Numbers were swapped that night at the In-N-Out; nobody has used them since. **At the party he stays at the grill.** When he sees the five of them together his face changes, and when he hears Yui's name near them he nearly sits down. **Add nothing to that. He has no lines tonight.** To the players he is just awkward.

**Detective Camila Ortiz.** SFPD Missing Persons; the detective on {{pc:1}}'s father's file. Late forties, sensible blazer, a coffee re-heated twice by noon. **Kinetic, professional, warm underneath but not performing warmth; uses your first name after the second sentence.** A month ago, at the police station, she wrote {{pc:4}} off as the idiot of the group — and then changed her mind out loud when he beat everybody to the meaning of the dates. **{{pc:4}} took her as a relationship when he advanced: she takes his calls now.** Nobody at the table has ever called her.

**The three people the party can call.** When they advanced, three of the players each chose a person rather than a skill: **{{pc:4}} has Ortiz, {{pc:2}} has Alaia Vega** (the ranger from Bodie), **and {{pc:1}} has Trina Vo** (a corporate investigator in Oakland with database access, who worked with him years ago). **These people come when the player calls them, not when the story needs them. Nobody has called one yet.** Tonight teaches that, once, with Ortiz. The other two are not prompted tonight: Alaia waits until after the next session, and Trina must never be pointed at {{pc:1}}'s father.

**Nia Chen.** The name on the index card that fell out of Bea's door. When they rang, she said yes to lunch and has named no day since. **About four weeks of polite not-yet.** Tonight she finally names one.

**Bea Ferro** — the archivist in the Outer Sunset — has not been seen since Thursday 11 February. That is a month now. Nothing about her happens tonight.

**Rosa Vega** — 79, Berkeley Hills, square dancer — keeps a ledger under her microwave and will not part with it. The party photographed every page. **They have been openly wondering whether to take the original.** Nothing tonight pushes that either way (Scene 7).

**Where they are.** **Coyote Point Recreation Area**, a county park on the Bay in San Mateo, just south of the airport. **The party is at the Bay Picnic Area**: a big reservable site with four charcoal grills, long tables, a lawn. **Magic Mountain playground is next door** — a wooden castle structure, six slides, purple dragons. **CuriOdyssey is across the park**, a small science museum and zoo of rescued, mostly native animals, including a **rubber boa**: a real, small, gentle snake the kids get to touch. It is windy. It is March. Everybody has a jacket they took off an hour ago and regrets.

## Scene 0 — The story so far, and one phone call · 10 minutes

### The recap — read it aloud · 3½ minutes

**Read the player recap page, as written.** It is the story so far, in their own terms, and it is safe for players: it tells them what they did and what they are still asking, and nothing they did not see. **Three weeks have passed at the table, and two weekly sessions were missed. They asked for this.**

**Do not add to it.** If a player asks "wait, what did the ledger say?", answer from what they photographed and move on.

### The drive down, and Ortiz · 5 minutes · **OFFER IT**

**Saturday, just after noon, on 101 South.** Tell the drive in two sentences, then open the door to the call. **Ortiz's contact card is already on the table in front of {{pc:4}}.**

> *"Saturday, just after noon, southbound on 101. The airport slides by on the left, planes stacked over the Bay. The birthday present is on somebody's lap. Unless somebody wants to stop me."*

**If anybody already called Ortiz this week, or calls now without being asked, that is the whole point. Say nothing about it and play the call.**

**If nobody does,** climb this ladder, one rung at a time, and stop the moment someone picks up the phone:

> **DM:** *"{{pc:4}}, it was your bag. You're about to meet the woman who got written up over it. Anything you want to walk in with?"*

> **DM:** *"You've got a detective who takes your calls now."*

> **DM:** *(last rung)* *"Want to call her? Just say 'I call Ortiz.'"*

**If still nothing, drop it.** No penalty, no comment; the card stays on the table.

**When the call happens,** Ortiz picks up on the third ring, in a car, with the indicator ticking.

> **ORTIZ:** *"{{pc:4}}. You actually called. Most people just save the number."*

**Let {{pc:4}} explain.** She listens without interrupting — which, for her, is a compliment. Then:

> **ORTIZ:** *"Okay. Not my lane, and I'm not a lawyer. But in California she can ask for her personnel file in writing, and they have thirty days. Her union rep will know the form. Tell her to ask for everything they used."*

**That is real law** (California Labor Code section 1198.5). **It is the whole of what Ortiz has.** If {{pc:4}} asks about anything else — Bea, the ledgers, {{pc:1}}'s father — she gives the short honest version:

> **ORTIZ:** *"That's {{pc:1}}'s case. He can call me. Have a good party."*

**She hangs up first. It is Saturday.**

**However {{pc:4}} talks to her — plainly or with the full performance — she answers briefly, and the same either way.** Tonight is not a test of how he talks; that is somebody else's job, soon.

Past it, on the lawn by the grills: balloons tied to a picnic table, fighting the wind. Unless somebody wants to stop me."*

## Scene 1 — Arrival · 12 minutes

**The Bay Picnic Area, one o'clock.** Thirty-odd guests: half of them airline crew in weekend clothes, the rest family and neighbours. Fifteen-odd children under six. Balloons. Four charcoal grills, one of them Reggie's. A folding table with a board of photographs on it: *Mei's first four years.* Wind off the water.

**Reggie sees them from the grill.** He lifts a pair of tongs in greeting and does not come over. **His face changes when he sees {{pc:4}}. It changes more when he sees all five of them together.** Leave it there.

**If {{pc:5}} is carrying the pistol:** the park is full of four-year-olds. Offer him the easy way out once, and only once:

> *"{{pc:5}}, you clock the car park, the bouncy castle, and roughly forty knee-height people. The lockbox is under your seat."*

**His call.** Whatever he decides, do not return to it.

**Yui finds them.** She is carrying a tray of lumpia and a juice box that has been squeezed empty. She puts both down to shake hands, properly, with each of them.

> **YUI:** *"You're the ones who asked. Reggie said you never once asked me what I did. Everybody else asked me what I did."*

**Right after the handshakes, before anybody asks, she closes the old question herself — kindly and completely:**

> **YUI:** *"I don't remember any of it. I've stopped trying. Come and eat."*

**If {{pc:4}} brings Ortiz's advice, this is where it lands.** Yui listens with her thumb on her cuff. Then she stops.

> **YUI:** *(only if he brings it)* *"They have to show me? In writing? …Nobody told me that. The union rep told me to let it go."*

**Let her be grateful in a small, specific way** — she writes it in her phone, she says his name back to him. **Nothing more comes of it tonight, and nothing needs to.**

**Mei arrives at knee height**, pulling on Yui's jeans. **Drop the first layer of her name here — both names, in one breath:**

> **YUI:** *"This is Mei. — Mei-Mei, say hi."*

**Mei-Mei does not say hi.** She stares at {{pc:5}}.

### {{pc:2}} and Yui · **FINE TO SKIP, but let it happen if it starts**

**{{pc:2}} will likely drift to Yui**; nurses find the tired parent. If she does, give them two minutes. 
> **YUI:** *(if asked how she is)* *"The purser job went to someone else. Mei was sick all winter — ear, chest, ear again. I haven't slept since she was born."*

**If {{pc:2}}'s player speaks to her in Mandarin, Yui switches at once and brightens.** It is the language she chose for herself. Two minutes in Mandarin between them is a gift; do not translate it for the table.

**If anybody asks about the name** — at any point tonight, not just here — **this is the whole answer. Do not explain further.**

> **YUI:** *(only if asked about the name)* *"My mom started it. Filipino thing — you double everything."*

## Scene 2 — Party jobs · 25 minutes · **everybody stands up**

**What happens:** within ten minutes of arriving, most of them have been given a job by somebody, the way people are at children's parties. **Get the players out of their chairs for this scene.** The table re-enacted a dance last time and it was the best part of the night.

### The snake · 1 minute

**Before anything else:** a grandmother took four of the older cousins across the park to CuriOdyssey an hour ago. One of them comes pelting back over the lawn, full volume:

> **COUSIN:** *"SNAAAAKE!"*

**Every adult on the lawn half-rises.** {{pc:2}} is probably first on her feet — let her be. **It is CuriOdyssey's rubber boa**: a real, small, gentle snake the kids get to hold, and she held it, and she is half terrified and half desperate to go back. **Everybody laughs.**

**This is a loud alarm that turns out to be nothing.** Later, the real emergency will make no sound at all. **Do not point at the contrast.**

### Simon Says — {{pc:4}} · **THEIR ONLY MOMENT**

**A harried aunt drafts {{pc:4}}**, because he is standing nearest and looks like a man who enjoys an audience:

> **AUNT:** *"You. You look like you could do Simon Says. A dozen of them. Please. Ten minutes."*

**The other four players are the toddlers.** Have them stand up. {{pc:4}}'s player runs the game; the table plays four-year-olds.

**The toddlers adore him.** Whatever he does, it works; they shriek, fall over, demand again. **This is a room where there is nobody to impress** — a four-year-old does not care about a speech, a theory or a voice — **and it goes well anyway.** Do not comment. Do not reward or punish any particular way of playing it.

**One round is enough if you are behind. Three is plenty if it is flying.**

### The piñata — {{pc:5}} · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**Then the piñata**: a papier-mâché dragon, purple, like the ones on the playground. Somebody hands {{pc:5}} the rope over a branch, because he is the nearest adult with both hands free.

**He holds a rope. That is all he does.** A blindfolded child swings; misses; the next child swings. **And between turns, the littlest ones — the two-year-olds, a three-year-old, and Mei-Mei — keep drifting away from the line and coming to stand against his legs.** Not playing. Just there. One of them holds his trouser leg. Their parents call them back. They come back.

**Hand {{pc:5}}'s player his slip now.** Say nothing with it.

> **{{pc:5}}'s slip:** *"You haven't said a word to them. You're just holding a rope. They keep coming back anyway."*

**Whatever he makes of it is his.** If he asks you what it means, shrug:

> **DM:** *(only if asked what it means)* *"Kids."*

 **If he tries to make it happen on purpose** — calls them over, does a voice — **it stops working**; they wander back to the piñata. Let him find that out.

**Keep {{pc:4}}'s toddlers with {{pc:4}}.** The children leaning on {{pc:5}} are the tiny ones who were never in the Simon Says game — **Mei-Mei included; she sat Simon Says out on Inez's lap.** **Do not let this look like the kids choosing one of them over the other.**

**His neck: say nothing now.** At pack-up, one line: *his neck is stiff tonight, the old ache.* Nothing more.

**If {{pc:5}} kept the pistol on him, keep the children at his hands and shoulders.** Do not make an event of it.

**If Mei-Mei gives him a name** — "rope man", "the tall one", his actual name — **write it down after the session.** It will come back.

### {{pc:3}} drifts to the photo board · 1 minute

**Last thing in this scene:** nobody has given {{pc:3}} a job, and the board of photographs is right there. **Point her at it and cut to Scene 3; the board is Scene 4.**

## Scene 3 — The cake · 8 minutes · **THEIR ONLY MOMENT** for {{pc:1}}

**The cake comes out.** As it does, one of Yui's Taipei colleagues — a woman in her fifties in a windbreaker — calls across the lawn to the birthday girl. **This is the second layer of her name, and the moment the Mandarin speakers at your table will catch it.** Say it with the falling tone, *MÈI-mei*, not the family's even "MAY-may":

> **TAIPEI COLLEAGUE:** *"妹妹，來吃蛋糕！"* — *(mèi-mei, lái chī dàn-gāo — "Little sister, come eat cake!")*

**Never explain it, never translate it, and never let anybody at the party comment on it.** If a player laughs, that is the whole payoff.

**The cake is enormous and correct in every way but one.** In blue piping across the top:

**Put the cake card on the table in front of {{pc:1}}, with the marker next to it.** It says **HAPPY 3rd BIRTHDAY MEI**. The delivery app reused last year's order. **And every adult who sees it agrees, warmly, that it's fine.**

> **AUNT:** *"Oh, that's fine. She can't read."*

> **GRANDFATHER:** *"Three, four. It's cake."*

**Nobody is lying and nobody means anything by it.** It is a small mistake a room has decided not to see.

**{{pc:1}} is holding a paper plate, standing next to it.** Offer the door, one rung at a time:

1. **The marker is right there next to the card.** Wait.
2. **Mei-Mei, on a chair to see over the table, frowning:**

> **MEI-MEI:** *"That's a three."*

3. **If he still doesn't, skip it** — keep the family question below.

**When he fixes it** — on the card, with the marker, however he likes — **nobody makes a fuss.** A grandparent still sings *"happy third birthday"* in the second verse. **And Mei-Mei jabs the corrected number with a finger:**

> **MEI-MEI:** *"FOUR!"*

**The room is still wrong and the one person who matters has the right number.** That is the beat.

### The family question · 30 seconds · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**While the cake is cut, a parent next to {{pc:1}} makes conversation**, the way parents at parties do:

> **PARENT:** *"Do you have family around here?"*

**He has to answer in front of people.** Whatever he says is the answer; the parent nods and passes him a plate. **No information, no lead, nothing follows.** If the player names a time, it is his; **you never say the phrase "seven years"**, and if you need a time yourself, use the month ("since October 2024").

## Scene 4 — The photo board · 15 minutes · **never cut** · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**What you need to know before this scene.** {{pc:3}} notices absences: in documents, records, photographs, she sees the shape of what has been taken out. **She has done it since Bodie, and once, after a second look at Bea's house, it gave her a mild headache.** She did not connect the two. Tonight, for the first time, the absence she reads belongs to **someone else's ordinary life** — not her brother's, not anything strange — **and the headache comes again.**

**The board.** Yui made it: *Mei's first four years*, forty photographs on foam board, pinned. Baby Mei. Mei in a pumpkin. Mei asleep on the airline's crew-room sofa. **And a baby-shower photograph where someone has been cut out** — a hand on Yui's shoulder, and then a clean edge where the rest of the person was.

**Open the scene by pointing her at it:**

> **DM:** *"{{pc:3}}, one of the photos on the board has somebody cut out of it."*

**This fires by default.** When she looks, she sees in the cut edge the shape of a woman about Yui's age, laughing, an arm round her. **Then hand her the slip:**

> **{{pc:3}}'s slip:** *"A dull headache starts behind your eyes."*

**It is a body cost, not a stress box: mark nothing.** It fades by morning. **Say nothing about why.**

**Yui comes over with cake for her**, sees what she is looking at, and says it plainly — **the human hand is explicit on purpose, so nobody at the table can read it as anything strange:**

> **YUI:** *"I cut her out. With scissors."*

**It was her closest friend. They fell out before Mei was born. Yui would rather it stayed cut.** That is where {{pc:3}} has to choose, out loud, between what she wants — to make the world hold what was removed — and what this person wants. **Do not resolve it for her.**

**If she presses, climb this ladder:**

1. **Yui changes the subject** — points at Mei in the pumpkin, asks if {{pc:3}} wants more cake.
2. **Yui says it plainly:**

> **YUI:** *(if {{pc:3}} presses once)* *"I'd rather not, if you don't mind."*

3. **Later, quietly, while they clear plates:**

> **YUI:** *(only if {{pc:3}} presses again)* *"We haven't spoken since before Mei was born. It's fine. It's done."*

**Whatever {{pc:3}} decides — to say the woman's shape aloud, to let it go, to ask one more question — that is the scene.** Yui does not get angry and does not give way. **She is at peace and she would like to stay there.**

**If {{pc:3}} never goes near the board at all,** Yui brings it to her: she carries it over to show off Mei in the pumpkin, and the cut photo is right there. Then run the scene as above.

## Scene 5 — The grape · 10 minutes · **never cut** · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**What happens:** after cake, Mei-Mei is sitting on the grass at {{pc:2}}'s knee, eating grapes from a bowl somebody left on the blanket. **She chokes. It makes no sound.** Choking children are silent; that is what makes it dangerous and what makes it land.

**Before you start: put the stuffed animal on the table. Keep your voice low.** The contrast with the snake is the whole scene.

**If anyone at your table looks genuinely upset at any point, the grape comes out now** — on the next thing anybody does — **and the howl follows immediately.** Nothing is lost by that.

**Hand {{pc:2}}'s player her slip. Say nothing. Wait for her to read it.**

> **{{pc:2}}'s slip:** *"Mei is making no sound. Her hands are at her throat. Nobody else has noticed."*

**Then, and only then, describe the lawn:** Yui is ten metres away, carrying the cake back to the table for a second round of candles. Reggie is at the grill. Everybody is talking. **Nobody else is looking.**

**No roll.** This does not depend on dice.

**{{pc:2}}'s player acts it out on the stuffed animal — never on another player.** Back blows, abdominal thrusts, whatever she knows; she is an ICU nurse, and she knows. **On her first thrust, the grape comes out** onto the grass. **A breath; then Mei howls** — loud, furious, perfectly healthy. **Crying means the airway is clear.** **Keep the silence right up to the howl, and cut away from the child's face.**

**If {{pc:2}} doesn't move,** climb, about five seconds per rung:

1. > **DM:** *"She's right at your knee. She's looking at you."*
2. > **YUI:** *(from across the lawn, the cake in her hands)* *"— I can't — you do it — "*
3. **If another PC acts, it works for them too** — first thrust, the grape, the howl. **{{pc:2}} gets the other slip-after** (below).

**If {{pc:2}} will not go at all,** another guest clears it. **Her moment tonight is then only the quiet afterwards** (Scene 6), which still runs.

**Meanwhile, offer {{pc:5}} the other children:**

> **DM:** *"{{pc:5}}, the other kids are drifting toward the blanket."*

**Whatever he does with that is his.**

**Yui gets there as it ends.** She has put the cake down on the table. She says one thing, to {{pc:2}}, with Mei-Mei in her arms:

> **YUI:** *"I froze. I've done that drill forty times at work and I froze."*

**If {{pc:2}} (or anybody) says the child should be checked by a doctor after thrusts** — which is correct — Yui agrees at once:

> **YUI:** *(if anyone says she should be checked)* *"I'm calling the paediatrician tonight. — Thank you."*

**The room smooths it over within a minute.** A grandparent says *"she's fine, she's fine, kids do that"*; someone relights a candle. **Let them.** Nobody at the party makes anything of it, and nobody at the party is wrong to be relieved.

**If a player asks you, out of character, whether that was magic:**

> **DM:** *(only if asked)* *"Kids are weird. Nurses are good."*

### {{pc:2}}'s second slip — hand it over when the room has moved on

> **{{pc:2}}'s slip, after:** *"It was out before your hands had finished moving. You are sure what you saw."*

**If another PC acted first,** use the other slip instead:

> **{{pc:2}}'s slip, after (if someone else got there first):** *"You were already moving."*

**This slip is about tonight only.** **Never add anything that connects it to the old woman in Sacramento or to the dance hall** — not in a slip, not in a line, not in a look. **If {{pc:2}}'s player makes that connection herself, that is hers; say nothing and let it stand.**

## Scene 6 — The quiet afterwards · 15 minutes · **never cut** · **HAS TO HAPPEN**

**What happens:** the party goes on. {{pc:2}} sits down somewhere near Yui. **Somebody puts a plate of cake in front of her and she can't eat it.** Tell her player that as a body fact, nothing more:

> *"You're not hungry. At all. It's the strangest thing."*

**A background parent brings a boy of six over from Magic Mountain**, snivelling. **He has a big, deep splinter** in the heel of his hand, off the wooden castle.

> **PARENT:** *"You're the nurse, right? Somebody said you're the nurse."*

**Offer it to her, and let her do it:**

> **DM:** *"There's a first-aid kit with tweezers on the table."*
