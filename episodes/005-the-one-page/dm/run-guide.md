## Session snapshot

**In-fiction date:** Monday, February 2027 — two days after the Saturday of Ep 4.

**Primary shape:** **bureaucratic puzzle** (a single page that means more than it says) + **interpersonal conflict** (the two-car split, named out loud). Deliberately *not* an archival session — no elder hands anyone a document in a quiet room. Three registers, three locations, none of them a records room. See [`../../../design/DM-ONLY/episode-outline.md`](../../../design/DM-ONLY/episode-outline.md) §Variety discipline.

**LOAD:** MEDIUM. The Bea scene that dominated the earlier draft is gone; Alaia replaces it.

**The three things that must land:**

1. **Ortiz in person**, the Argonaut page, and the seam acknowledged-not-explained. (U2 · S3 institutional-frame breadcrumb)
2. **Alaia Vega, in person.** Ep 4 closed on her *"see you in Berkeley soon"*; this pays it one session later. (§35 · B2 partial)
3. **Nia Chen's name enters the party's possession** via a card in Bea's door. (**A1 planted** — the single most overdue item in the campaign.)

**Everything else is negotiable.**

## Standing DM knowledge

### The dates — memorise these, do not improvise them

Per [`facts-ledger.md`](../../../design/DM-ONLY/facts-ledger.md) §6 *Canonical date set (LOCKED)*:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Feb 2018** | Someone queries Argonaut's dissolved CA filings. Morgan's father's SSN cross-matches off a 1994 filing. Margin note: *"ARH → follow up."* Nobody follows up. |
| **autumn 2024** | The missed birthday. Last direct contact. |
| **Feb 2025** | Morgan files SFPD Missing Persons. Ortiz assigned. |
| **Feb 2027** | Now. A routine two-year cold-case sweep re-runs the cross-match; the 2018 hit surfaces with it. |

**9 years** = Feb 2018 → now. This is the number in the voicemail the players heard, and in the recap they were emailed. It is correct and immovable.
**7 years** = Feb 2018 → Feb 2025. **This is the seam.**
**2 years** = roughly what Morgan has lived since his father went quiet.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Atticus caught this at the Ep 4 table and he was right.** Let him be right. Do not correct him, do not deflect, do not go number-free. Say the dates.

**Speak absolute months, never intervals** — *"February of '18," "February of '25."* Intervals drift as in-game time advances; the absolute dates never move.

### What you are holding back

The committed reading is **(a) father-was-already-in-motion**: the 2018 cross-match generated chatter, a note reached his telecom-security-adjacent colleagues, one warned him privately, and he spent six and a half years arranging. His disappearance was the completion, not the beginning. **This is a mundane human story about silence-under-threat. Never voice it.** Ortiz acknowledges the seam and stops.

Who ran the 2018 query stays opaque. Bea's possible role in it is held for Ep 7+ — **and this is why she is not home in Scene 4.** Ortiz saying *"someone ran a query in 2018"* and Bea saying *"I feel like I've heard that name"* ninety minutes apart, at a table that cannot firewall player-knowledge from PC-knowledge, *is* the reveal. Keep them apart.

### Bea, if the party asks about her

She does not explain. Ever. Per [`../../002-the-archivists-index/dm/the-archivist.md`](../../002-the-archivists-index/dm/the-archivist.md): *"Bea shows records and never draws the conclusion… Recognition is fine and chilling; interpretation breaks the invariant."* She may show, hand over, mail, or point. She may not interpret.

She is also, per DM ruling, **allowed to be elusive** — recurring but hard to pin down. The discipline that keeps this from reading as a missing NPC: **absent in body, never absent in effect.** Every session she ghosts, she still delivers something physical. This session it is a card in a door.

### The ledger count, if it comes up

**1 (Bea) + 3 (Dakota's, from the Sacramento locker) + 4 unfound = 8.** The four unfound are Palo Alto, Bancroft, San Bernardino, Central Valley. Do not let anyone — NPC or DM — recite this arithmetic aloud this session; it is not the session's business, and the party doing the arithmetic themselves is worth more than being told. If a player does the sum correctly, confirm the total and nothing else.

## Scene 1 — SFPD Bay Station

**Budget: 30–35 min.** Register: **urban police-institutional, kinetic.** Ortiz does not sit and slide a page across a desk. She walks.

### Setup

Ortiz called Morgan back — **she initiates, not him.** (Ep 4's played record only says Morgan *may* have called between sessions, and he is the party's most reticent PC on this topic. Do not build the session's anchor scene on an offscreen action. If Atticus says Morgan did call, great; if not, Ortiz left a message Sunday night saying she'd found something and would be at her desk Monday.)

**Not everyone gets to come in.** Missing Persons is not a room five civilians sit in. Morgan plus one other at Ortiz's desk; the rest wait in the lobby. Run the lobby in parallel — three short beats, then reconvene.

### The lobby beats *(3–4 min total, run these first)*

- **Mars and the metal detector.** Mars carries concealed, and per his sheet a government building is exactly where that becomes *real logistical friction, not a moral problem.* He knows this going in. Does he leave it in the truck, declare it at the desk, or not come inside? Ninety seconds, entirely in-character, and it is the session's most-parked player's best texture. **Do not skip this.**
- **Priya** notices something clinical about a woman waiting with a folder — the particular stillness of someone who has been waiting a long time and expects to keep waiting.
- **Dakota** does what Dakota does to a desk sergeant.

### Beat 1 — The one page *(12–15 min)*

Ortiz walks Morgan back through the bay — third row from the window, a bulletin board with a coffee-stain across the corner of every fifth flyer — talking while moving, interrupted twice. She hands the page over **standing up**.

It is a property-title cross-match. Someone in 2018 pulled the ownership history of a Sacramento self-storage unit and, in the same query, cross-matched every SSN in the chain of ownership filings.

**On the page:**

- The storage-unit address — **the same one Dakota went to.** Let them make that connection; it is the only "discovery" beat in the scene that is actually still available.
- Chain of ownership: registered to **Argonaut Records Holdings, LLC**, 1988–1998. Dissolved 1998. Pre-paid facility fee runs through **2035**. No successor entity.
- Cross-matched SSNs, redacted except one: **Morgan's father**, against a **1994 filing naming him "technical consultant, authorized access."**

> [!NOTE]
> The name **Argonaut is not a reveal any more.** Dakota already asked Vivian about it at the Ep 4 table. Do not budget suspense for the party recognising it — they will name it in three seconds. **The new thing on this page is Morgan's father's signature on a 1994 Argonaut filing**, and that is what the beat is about: the personal thread and the institutional thread turning out to be the same piece of paper.

When it lands, Ortiz notices the recognition and does not press:

> *"…you know that name. OK. Tell me what you know."*

Let the party explain, or refuse to. She listens. **This is Beck's scene as much as Morgan's** — Dakota is the one who found Argonaut, and "tell me what you know" in front of a cop, with Morgan needing her cooperation, is exactly where politician-speech is either a weapon or a liability. There is a decision in this and it costs nothing to stage.

### Beat 2 — The seam *(10–12 min)* — Morgan's beat

**If Atticus raises it himself** (likely — he caught it in Ep 4), use this:

> She pulls the page back toward her, checks it, slides it back.
>
> *"…yeah. February '18, February '25. You did that in your head faster than I did it on paper. Seven years.*
>
> *Nobody flagged it at the time — that's in the file too, and that's the part that actually bothers me. Not that it's seven years. That nobody thought seven years was worth a phone call."*

**If he doesn't**, raise it yourself at ~7 minutes in:

> *"You caught that? Good — most people don't read the dates.*
>
> *The query's February of '18. I opened your father's file February of '25. Seven years apart, near enough. And nine to today, which is the only reason it surfaced at all — two-year cold-case sweep re-ran the cross-match and the old hit came up with it.*
>
> *So. Seven years between somebody pulling your father's number in Sacramento and your father going quiet the autumn before I opened this file.* (beat) *I've worked cases where a number gets pulled and it means nothing at all. I've worked one where it was the whole thing. I can't tell you which this is. I've been looking at those two dates since Friday and I don't have anything to hang on them."*

Then, quieter, off the record:

> *"For what it's worth — somebody at the records office made a note on the 2018 query. Not an official flag. Handwritten, in the margin of the file. 'ARH → follow up.' Nothing followed up. But somebody was paying attention."*

**Delivery:** *"seven years, near enough."* Never *"exactly seven years to the day."* A tired detective is precise; a tired detective **emphasising** precision is the author talking.

**Do not reveal:** who wrote the margin note; who ran the 2018 query; anything about reading (a).

### Beat 3 — S1 flattening #1 *(90 sec)*

Deploy during Beat 1 or 2. **Put it on a PC, not past the table** — Mars (or whoever is in the room) notices it and says so out loud.

> A dispatcher passing Ortiz's desk, not looking at the paperwork: *"Hey — log that as a cold-lead follow-up on the '25 file, not the '18 file. The app grouped them under '25 automatically. It's what I would've done too."*
>
> Ortiz, mild frown: *"…yeah. OK."*

The '18 file was the query; the '25 file was the disappearance. Grouping them under '25 **loses the seven-year gap in the record** — the exact thing Ortiz just said bothers her, erased from the file ninety seconds later by a default nobody chose.

If a PC objects, the dispatcher is friendly and unmoved and the software has already done it. Ortiz half-nods and moves on.

**Never say "that's flattening."** Note the party's reaction, or lack of one, privately.

> [!WARNING]
> **Rate discipline.** One per session, and every instance must be a *default, a dropdown, an app, or a tired human agreeing with one* — **never a choice a person makes.** The moment it reads as a person deleting things on purpose, the party has a villain, and hook S3 exists specifically to prevent that. If the party says *"someone is doing this"* twice, stop deploying for two sessions and let it cool.

### Beat 4 — Departure *(3 min)*

Ortiz gives Morgan her direct line and says she'll pull the full Argonaut filing trail over the week.

**Then start the joke.** She will be almost impossible to reach from here until Ep 11. Every subsequent contact is a real voicemail — use the audio assets. Phone tag with a stranger is a shrug; phone tag with a detective they like, who is fighting her own caseload to keep this file open, is funny *and* quietly sad.

*(The outside-law-firm plant, B7, moves to Ep 9. Do not deploy it here — this scene is full.)*

## Scene 2 — Two cars, again

**Budget: 15 min.** Register: moving vehicles, crossing the bridge east.

### Beat 1 — The split, named *(4–5 min)* — interpersonal

They travel split again — **out of habit, not out of a decision.** Nobody re-negotiated it after Saturday.

**Let a player notice, and if nobody does, have the most-affected PC notice at ~3 minutes.** *"We're doing it again."* Whether they consolidate is entirely theirs. Do not push.

This is the Ep-4 loose end promoted into a beat instead of quietly reverted. Note also that the earlier draft put all five in one SUV, which would have silently undone the party state Ep 4 actually ended on.

### Beat 2 — Mira reads the notebook *(8–10 min)* — Mira's beat

Mira has been carrying Loewe's notebook — or asks for it now.

> **Prompt Beck, not Dakota:** *"Beck — Vivian put that in your hands on Saturday. Ariadne's reaching for it. What does Dakota do?"*

*(Meta prompts go to the player; in-fiction narration goes to the character. Never mix.)*

Open to the excised cluster, pages 47–52.

**Deliver Mira's read PRIVATELY** — a note passed, a step into the kitchen, a text. Not aloud. Per [`principles.md`](../../../design/DM-ONLY/principles.md) §3 and `rules.md` §Realization mechanics, per-PC recognition is private; and Beat 3 below is Ariadne's decision to make, which the DM pre-empts by narrating it to the room.

- **Free tier, no roll:** *"You sense the negative-space of what was there. Something diagrammatic — geometric — arranged. You can't tell what."*
- **Insight 10+:** *"Something like six shapes in an arrangement — a cluster, or a lineup, or a hierarchy — you can't tell which."*
- **Insight 12+:** *"Six figures in what feels like a network — lines between them, some structural relationship — the geometry has meaning but the meaning is opaque."*

> [!CAUTION]
> **STOP AT 12+.** No face-recognition, no party-shape-match, no Kai-adjacent breadcrumb. Per [`facts-ledger.md`](../../../design/DM-ONLY/facts-ledger.md) §21, the retrocausal-self reading must be **player-voiced first**, and no player has voiced it. Six figures, geometry only.

### Beat 3 — Share or hold *(2–3 min)*

Ariadne decides whether Mira tells the party. Both are in character. **And the people in the other car don't hear it either way** — which is the split's cost, made concrete, without anyone having to moralise about it.

## Scene 3 — Berkeley, a coffee counter

**Budget: 35–40 min.** Register: **peer, urban, ordinary.** This is the session's payoff and its emotional centre.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Alaia is the first non-elder network contact in the campaign.** Bea, Rosa and Vivian are all elders. Per her hard invariant, do **not** stage her as another elder-in-a-home scene. Her venues are workplace, text thread, or peer-appropriate coffee. She is a few years older than the PCs. The point of her existing is that the network is not just old librarians — there are people the PCs' own age in it, quietly.

### Setup

She texted Morgan *"see you in Berkeley soon"* on Saturday. She is down from Bodie visiting her mother. She picks the place — a counter with four stools, not a table for six, so the party has to stand around and rotate. Good.

**Voice:** slightly-clipped Bay-Area cadence. Gives the shorter answer first and the longer one only if asked twice. Warmth is real but rationed. Uses names sparingly and correctly. Does not perform authority.

### Beat 1 — She has been paying attention *(10 min)*

She knows things she shouldn't quite know — not ominously, just a beat more than a park ranger who met them once. She asks a question that reveals she has been logging them, without ever saying so. Let the party notice.

**The QR codes close here.** If Morgan raises them (he clocked they were personalized in Ep 3), she is straightforwardly honest: yes, the cards carry per-visitor suffixes; yes, that is how she knew they came back to Building 7; no, it is not the park's idea. **U3 resolves** — the orphaned QR channel becomes the thing that already worked, which is better than it quietly disappearing.

### Beat 2 — What she gives *(15 min)*

Three things, and no more:

1. **The referral.** *"My mother has her own work. She's up the hill. Go and knock — but call first, she hates a surprise."* This is the Ep 6 hook, delivered peer-to-peer rather than by an elder's cryptic instruction.
2. **The crack.** Once, quietly, not repeated: *"There are more of us than you think. And fewer than you'd want."*
3. **A boundary.** She will not talk about her mother's past, and says so plainly rather than deflecting.

> [!CAUTION]
> **She does NOT reveal the Hidden faction.** That is Ep 11 (B2 full). Pulling her *meeting* forward pays the cliffhanger; pulling her *reveal* forward spends a card the campaign needs later. She confirms nothing about the network's structure, names no one, and does not use the word.
>
> **She also does not name Nia.** Nia's arrival comes through Bea's card in Scene 4, and the Act I→II hinge line is Nia's own in Ep 8.

### Beat 3 — Leaving *(5–8 min)*

She pays for her own coffee, says something small and warm, and goes. No cliffhanger — this session's ending belongs to the card in Bea's door.

## Scene 4 — Bea, missed

**Budget: 5 min.** Do not let this expand.

They detour through the Outer Sunset on the way back. **She is not home.** The house is dark; the kettle is not on; a neighbour hasn't seen her since Thursday.

Wedged in the storm door, at eye height, where it could not be missed and could not have been there long:

> An index card. Bea's hand. A name and a phone number.
>
> **Nia Chen.**
>
> Nothing else. No note, no explanation, no indication of when it was left or how she knew they would come.

**That is the entire scene.** Do not have her come home. Do not have her call. Do not explain the card.

**Why this works and the 55-minute kitchen scene didn't:** it plants A1 — the campaign's most overdue hook, fire-by "late Ep 4 or Ep 5" — *through Bea*, which is canonical; it honors her hard invariant absolutely (she shows; she does not interpret; she is not even present to be asked); it uses the elusiveness the DM sanctioned; and it costs five minutes instead of fifty-five.

**If a player asks how she knew:** she isn't there to ask. That is the point. Log any theories privately.

## Rolls, and the ones I removed

**Keep:**

- **Mira, Insight, notebook.** Canonical escalation; hard cap at 12+. If she pushes a third time in the scene, the rapid-cast stress check is **2d6 + WIS** per [`rules.md`](../../../world/rules.md) §Magic — *no +2*, and note the rule is triggered by repeated **casts**, not repeated Insight rolls. If you want a cost for the third push, rule it explicitly as a house call rather than misciting the mechanic.
- **Dakota, Influence,** if he tries to manage Ortiz during *"tell me what you know."* Genuine branch: 10+ she takes the party seriously as sources rather than as Morgan's entourage; 6- she starts addressing only Morgan for the rest of the scene.

**Removed from the earlier draft:**

- *Morgan Insight to read whether Ortiz is holding back.* All three outcome tiers meant "no." A roll whose only function is to confirm a read is a "yes" you can simply narrate.
- *Dakota Influence for the law-firm name*, which specified *"she declines regardless."* A roll with a predetermined outcome teaches players that rolling doesn't matter. The B7 plant moved to Ep 9 anyway.

## Wild outcomes — pre-decided

This session caps Mira's notebook read hard, and `rules.md` hands **double 6s** to the *player* to narrate an unexpected positive detail. Left unplanned, that rule hands Ariadne narration rights over precisely the thing the firewall forbids. So decide now:

- **Double 6s on the notebook read:** the grant is spent on **provenance, not content.** She can tell the cut was made with a fresh blade, in a warm room, unhurried — that the paper is pre-war, that the excision was done by someone who was not in a hurry and did not want it to look violent. Rich, true, and reveals nothing about the six figures.
- **Double 1s:** pre-loaded complication, not an improvised reveal. The binding cracks and a page comes loose; or the car's navigation reroutes them and they lose fifteen minutes; or Priya's phone rings at the worst moment.

## Contingencies

- **Morgan says he never called Ortiz.** Covered — she initiates. Do not build on an offscreen action.
- **The party won't split for the lobby.** Ortiz comes out to the lobby and runs the scene standing in a public waiting area, which is worse for her and better for the table. Fine.
- **They skip Berkeley.** Don't let them, gently — Alaia texts again mid-drive and names a time. If they genuinely refuse, she turns up at the Ocean Beach beach-fire in Ep 7 instead, and Ep 6's Rosa hook comes from Vivian's mailed letter file instead of from Alaia.
- **They skip Bea's.** The card is mailed and arrives Wednesday. **A1 gets planted this week regardless** — it is the one thing in this session that cannot slip again.
- **A player voices the retrocausal-self theory** (*"what if Loewe drew US on that page?"*): do **not** confirm. *"Interesting. What does Mira do with that thought?"* Log the volunteer privately — it is the prerequisite for the six-figures reveal per `facts-ledger.md` §21.
- **A player asks whether all three archivists lived in the same house.** They can be told yes, if they've worked it out — that is now canon and Bea would not deny it. What Bea will never do is explain how three consecutive people came to acquire one house. Hold the mundane mechanism (a small family landlord selling to sitting tenants; a probate routing through the same office) in reserve and deploy it **only if a player digs**.

## If you are running long — the pre-designated cut

Decide now, not at 10pm. **In order:**

1. **Scene 4 (Bea's card) → mailed, arrives Wednesday.** Costs 5 minutes and loses nothing; A1 still plants.
2. **Scene 2 Beat 2 (the notebook read) → Ep 6's drive.** U7 has already waited a session; one more is survivable.
3. **Scene 1's lobby beats.** Cheapest to cut, but it costs Mars his only content — cut this *last*, not first.

**Never cut:** Ortiz's seam beat (Beat 2), or Alaia. Those are the session.

## If you are running short — the extension module

The old draft said *"if the session finishes early, end early."* Against a 2.5h norm that plans for the wrong failure. If you have 20–30 minutes spare:

- **Morgan calls Ortiz back** and actually reaches her — she has pulled two more Argonaut filings, one of which names a second consultant whose name means nothing yet.
- **Rosa answers the phone** when they call ahead, and is briskly unhelpful in a way that makes Ep 6 more interesting.
- **A Priya private beat** — she rereads her own notes on Vivian's pulse and finds she has been writing down more than she meant to.

**Do not reach into Ep 6's material.** Bancroft and Rosa's garden are next week's session.

## Post-session workflow

1. **Collect advancement marks.** Each player may mark one, per [`rules.md`](../../../world/rules.md) §Advancement. **This was missed at the end of Ep 4** and is now a standing item — several PCs are plausibly owed marks toward a first advancement. Record them in the session log's `marks` field.
2. Write `sessions/<date>/session.json` against the `session-log` schema, plus `summary.md` for the players. Run `quire lint .` before committing.
3. Fold the played record into this file as a `## Played record (<date>)` section at the top, per the Ep 4 pattern.
4. Update `hook-ledger.md` fire statuses. Expected fires: U2, §35/B2-partial, U3 closed, **A1 planted**, S1 #1, U7 partial, T4/U6.
5. Add played facts to `facts-ledger.md`, and re-snapshot as `continuity-through-ep-5.md`.
6. Update `episode-outline.md`: Ep 5 → PLAYED; confirm Ep 6's shape.
7. **Log Mars's A7 deferral explicitly** and confirm his private beat is scheduled for Ep 7.
8. **Check Vivian's clock.** She must appear in person by Ep 10. If Ep 6's mail beat lands well, resist the temptation to mail again.
