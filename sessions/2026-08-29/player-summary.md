# Session 5 — The One Page

**Monday 15 February 2027.** Four days after Bodie, two after Sacramento.

---

## Park Station

Detective Ortiz asked you to come in — not to headquarters, but to the district
station at Waller and Stanyan, a 1910 building put up for the mounted police,
where the doorways are too wide because they were built for horses. Mars offered
Dakota a lift and arranged to collect him from Fisherman's Wharf, which is four
and a half miles from a police station Dakota could have walked to in fifteen
minutes. She kept you ten minutes in the lobby and had you out inside twenty.

**She had one page.** In February 2018, somebody paid a records vendor to pull
Argonaut Records Holdings' dissolved business filings — and in the same request,
cross-matched every Social Security number that had ever appeared in them. One
name came back unredacted: **Daniel Reyes, "technical consultant, authorized
access," 1994.** Morgan's father, on a document nobody in this room had ever seen
him near.

**Mira saw what was wrong with it.** You query a dead company to check whether
it is dead. You do not pull the people out of it. *Somebody wasn't checking a
company. Somebody was looking for a person.* Ortiz had never had a reason to
check who ordered it. She checked. **Sutter Document Services**, Sacramento,
dissolved 2021 — and their billing account is stamped on the corner of a page
you had been holding for ten minutes. The request log does not say who paid. It
never does. That is the product.

**Then Dakota put his own copy on the desk.** The original names the entry Ortiz's
page has blacked out — **H. ODEGAARD** — which is a redaction beaten with a
document you went and got yourselves. It also has *two* entries where hers has
three. Hers carries a third, dated **1991**, listed as *agent*. His has no gap
where it would go. Both documents are real.

**And then a clerk fixed it.** Mateo noticed the 2018 record was filed under the
wrong case number, tidied it in under the 2025 case, and was thanked for it. He
was right. He fixed a genuine error. The seven-year gap that made Ortiz pick up
the phone after two years now sits inside one continuous file, and she is
satisfied the case is mostly uninteresting.

*Mars and Morgan took his coffee. Mira had chamomile. Dakota wanted a double
espresso and there wasn't one.*

## Caffè Strada

The ranger from Bodie bought her own coffee and then bought everyone else's.

**Dakota told her everything**, at considerable length, including a theory about
an insurance company that she could not follow at all. One word got through:
**ledger.** She asked to see a picture of one. He showed her.

*She had seen a book like that once. On her mother's kitchen table. Years ago.*

She called her mother from the table. **Rosa Vega** put you on hold by putting
the receiver down without covering it, worked audibly through a paper calendar —
square dancing, church group, doctor's appointments — offered Saturday, said
**"8:20am sharp"**, and hung up without giving an address. Alaia laughed, gave
you the address, and said get there by 8:15 if you want to impress her.

**Alaia bought the round** because she was struck by what Priya does for a
living. They ordered for everybody and discovered they both take a plain
Americano. Mars got single-origin drip and admitted it was not as bad as
feared, though he wanted matcha. Mira got a chai and was delighted. Dakota
received the largest pumpkin spice latte on the menu, with extra cream, caramel
fudge and heart-shaped rainbow sprinkles, and loved it without irony.

**Dakota handed Mira the notebook.** She photographed every page — on the theory
that the number of them might change when nobody is looking — and then noticed
her count did not match his. Hers ran past 150, his about twenty short. She
worked it out herself: he had counted files, she had read page numbers, and the
difference is everywhere pages have been cut out. **The largest gap is the six at
the centre.**

Reading the absence, she found **six shapes, arranged in some relationship to
each other, and writing underneath.** Pressing harder made it go soft. Alaia
concluded this was some new-age thing young people do and ignored it.

Then Alaia asked what actually happened in Building 7 — and mid-sentence, took a
call she did not explain and left.

## The Outer Sunset

Ren texted the group somewhere on the Bay Bridge, trying to be cool. It was not.

**Bea was not home.** The house was dark, the kettle cold, and a neighbour had
not seen her since Thursday. Mira could not get the front door — a basic Schlage,
sticky old pins. Dakota shouted his business through it. Morgan hammered on it.

**Dakota found the kitchen window, left open for the cat**, went through, and
opened the door from the inside — **and an index card fell out of the frame.** In
Bea's square unhurried capitals:

> **NIA CHEN — (415) 555-0163**

The house held shoeboxes of index cards, filed on a system nobody could crack:
not by date, not by subject, not alphabetical, but colour-coded in an elaborate
scheme of extremely muted colours, which Dakota took as a personal affront.

**The cat had opinions.** It cornered Dakota, declined to confirm whether it was
Bea, and herded everyone back to the kitchen until Mira fed it. Dakota left Bea a
long note explaining a complicated plan for why she should talk to him again.
Mira added: *more hot pink please.*

**You called the number.** Nia texted back and said yes to lunch, at no
particular time, and none of you can tell whether you have been talking to
someone your own age or someone Bea's.
