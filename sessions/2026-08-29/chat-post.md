**Session 5 — The One Page**

Monday morning. Before any of it started, {{pc:5}} offered {{pc:4}} a lift and
told him to wait at Fisherman's Wharf. {{pc:5}} did not come. {{pc:4}} paid for a
Waymo across the entire city to a police station **0.7 miles from his own front
door** and arrived five minutes after everybody else. This is now a matter of
public record.

**Ortiz had one page.** In February 2018 somebody paid a records vendor to pull a
dead company's filings — and in the same request, cross-matched every Social
Security number that had ever appeared in them. One name came back unredacted.
**{{pc:1}}'s father**, on a 1994 filing: *technical consultant, authorized
access.*

**{{pc:3}} was the one who saw what was wrong with it.** You query a dead company
to check whether it's dead. You don't pull the *people* out of it. Somebody
wasn't checking a company — somebody was looking for a person. Ortiz had never
had a reason to check who ordered the thing. So she checked: **Sutter Document
Services**, Sacramento, dissolved 2021, and their billing code was stamped on the
corner of the page you'd all been holding for ten minutes.

**Then {{pc:4}} put his own copy down**, and it named the entry hers had blacked
out — **H. ODEGAARD**. It also has *two* entries where hers has *three*; hers
carries a third from 1991 that his original has no room for. Neither document is
a fake.

{{pc:4}} also offered Ortiz his full theory of the case — that every archivist
has worked for the same insurance company, that the company is the thing behind
all of this, and that Bea keeps her records off-site so they can never become its
official records. **Ortiz cut him off**: too much, and none of it relevant to what
she was doing. She offered to pass notes to other departments. Nobody at the
table has actually disproved any of it.

**And then a clerk fixed it.** Mateo noticed the 2018 record was filed under the
wrong case number and tidied it into the right one. He was correct. He fixed a
real error. Ortiz thanked him, and now considers the case pretty much nothing.

**Berkeley.** Alaia bought the whole round, because she was impressed by what
{{pc:2}} does for a living — and it turns out they both take a plain Americano.
{{pc:4}} received the largest pumpkin spice latte in Alameda County and loved it
without irony. {{pc:5}} got single-origin drip and would still rather have had
matcha.

{{pc:4}} told Alaia everything too, and one word got through: **ledger.** He
showed her a picture. *She'd seen one. On her mother's kitchen table. Years ago.*

She didn't ring her mother — she handed over the number and told you to do it
yourselves. {{pc:4}} called. **Rosa Vega** put him on hold without covering the
receiver, audibly worked through a paper calendar re: square dancing, church
group and a doctor's appointment, offered Saturday, said **"8:20am sharp"** and
hung up without giving an address.

**{{pc:3}} then out-detectived everyone.** She photographed every page of the
notebook — on the theory that the number of pages might change when nobody's
looking — noticed her count didn't match {{pc:4}}'s, got briefly and completely
excited, and then worked out the answer herself before anyone could help: he'd
counted files, she'd read page numbers, and the gap is everywhere pages have been
cut out. The largest is the six missing from the middle. Reading that absence:
**six shapes, arranged in some relationship, and writing underneath.** Alaia
decided this was a new-age thing young people do.

**Then Bea's house, and Bea wasn't in it.** {{pc:3}} couldn't get the lock.
{{pc:4}} shouted his business through the door. {{pc:1}} hammered on it. {{pc:4}}
found the kitchen window left open for the cat, climbed through, and opened the
door from the inside — **and an index card fell out of the frame:**

> **NIA CHEN — (415) 555-0163**

Shoeboxes of index cards in there, colour-coded on a system nobody could crack
and which {{pc:4}} found aesthetically offensive. The cat declined to confirm
whether it was Bea, and herded everyone to the kitchen until {{pc:3}} fed it.

You called the number. Nia said yes to lunch, at no particular time, and nobody
is sure whether you've been talking to someone your age or someone Bea's.

pleased with a piece of prep in my life.)*
