**Session 5 — The One Page** 🗂️

Monday morning, Park Station. Ortiz had one page and twenty minutes.

**The page:** in Feb 2018 someone paid a records vendor to pull a dead company's
filings — and in the same breath cross-matched every SSN that had ever appeared
in them. One name came back unredacted. **Morgan's dad**, on a 1994 filing,
"technical consultant, authorized access."

**Mira spotted what was wrong with it:** you query a dead company to check it's
dead. You don't pull the *people* out of it. Somebody wasn't checking a company.
Somebody was looking for a person. Ortiz had never had a reason to check who
ordered it — so she checked. **Sutter Document Services**, Sacramento, dissolved
2021, and their billing code was stamped on the corner of the page you'd been
holding for ten minutes.

**Then Dakota put his own copy down**, and it named the entry hers had blacked
out — **H. ODEGAARD**. It also has *two* entries where hers has *three*. Both
documents are real. 🤨

**And then a clerk fixed it.** Mateo noticed the 2018 record was under the wrong
case number and tidied it into the right one. He was correct. He fixed a real
error. Ortiz thanked him, and now thinks the case is pretty much nothing.

☕ **Berkeley.** Alaia bought the whole round because she was impressed by what
Priya does for a living — turns out they both take a plain Americano. Dakota
received the largest pumpkin spice latte in Alameda County with sprinkles, and
loved it. Mars got single-origin drip and would still rather have had matcha.

Dakota told Alaia everything, and one word got through: **ledger**. He showed her
a picture. *She'd seen one. On her mother's kitchen table. Years ago.*

She rang her mum on the spot. **Rosa Vega** put you on hold without covering the
receiver, audibly consulted a paper calendar re: square dancing and church group,
said **"8:20am sharp"** and hung up — without giving an address. 😅

Mira photographed every page of the notebook (theory: the number of pages might
change when nobody's looking) and found where six had been cut out of the middle.
Reading the gap: **six shapes, arranged in some relationship, and writing
underneath.** Alaia decided this was a new-age thing young people do.

🌊 **Then Bea's house — and Bea wasn't home.** Dark, cold kettle, neighbour hadn't
seen her since Thursday. Mira couldn't get the lock. Dakota found the window left
open for the cat, climbed through, opened the door from inside — **and an index
card fell out of the frame:**

> **NIA CHEN — (415) 555-0163**

Shoeboxes of index cards inside, colour-coded on a system none of you could crack
and which Dakota found aesthetically offensive. The cat declined to confirm
whether it was Bea, and herded everyone to the kitchen until Mira fed it. 🐈

You called the number. Nia said yes to lunch, at no particular time. Nobody is
sure whether you've been talking to someone your age or someone Bea's.
