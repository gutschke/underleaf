# Session 4 recap — "The Sacramento Visit"

*For all five players — safe to share in the group chat.*

## At Dakota's apartment

You met at Dakota's place at noon. Three ledgers on his dining table. Priya arrived last and stayed standing. The room had a lot to say.

Dakota did share the story — Bea, the Sacramento self-storage locker, an LLC that dissolved in 1998, three more ledgers annotated by a woman named **Eleanor Marsh** (Bea's predecessor, died 1987), a margin note pointing at Vivian Loewe — but he told it the way Dakota tells things: circuitously, with detours, and with an air that suggested he was measuring what to disclose. Everyone in the room clocked it. **Mira** cut in, unexpectedly and with force — she doesn't do that often, and Dakota registered the hit. Priya held her anger closer to her chest than she might have liked, but held it. Morgan tried to redirect by playing **Detective Ortiz's voicemail** about his father — a Sacramento paperwork echo on his SSN nine years ago — but Dakota looped the conversation back to himself within a minute. Priya reminded the room that a 98-year-old woman was expecting a call, and everyone agreed to go.

Dakota called Vivian's caregiver **Elena** — she was noncommittal (Vivian was still asleep) and didn't promise anything for today. Dakota told the party to get moving anyway.

## The drive

Two cars, because several of you refused to share one with Dakota. **Mars and Dakota** took Mars's pickup. **Morgan, Priya, and Mira** rode in Dakota's car. On the way, **Priya's migraine flared** near Vacaville — same pressure she'd had Wednesday. Bad enough that Morgan and Mira asked; not bad enough to stop. Morgan and Dakota kept at each other for most of the drive, with Dakota giving a masterclass in *politician speech* — talking freely about how "this is so much bigger" while conveying no additional information, and appearing genuinely puzzled that this frustrated anyone. Mars played Dakota's Bodie voicemail over the car speakers to see if hearing it again would help. It did not.

## At Vivian's

Both cars pulled up together. Vivian's place is a small 1960s ranch with a well-kept fig tree. **Mira noticed something odd about the front walk** — hard to place, and she kept it to herself.

**Elena met you on the porch.** She recognized Dakota but registered four unfamiliar faces and hesitated — Vivian had had a rough morning. Mars tried to work the door; the first attempt didn't take (Priya spoke over him at exactly the wrong moment). Mars regrouped, leaned casually on the railing, told Elena he'd honestly rather drink from a garden hose than intimidate her, and then quietly named the real thing — he'd driven hours and needed to see this old woman for himself, whether or not Dakota was making the whole thing up. Elena softened. She let all five of you in on a short leash.

## The interview

Priya and Morgan read the opening as friendly small talk. Mars, Mira, and Dakota picked up sooner: **Vivian was assaying you.** She asked light personal questions (how you met — "on a plane" — where you work, what you're reading) and listened carefully.

A few threads that came out of that hour:

- Vivian was **surprised Priya wasn't Taiwanese** — she'd assumed from the plane story. Vivian is 98; she noticed with the casual bluntness of someone who has watched a lot of change and doesn't pretty things up. When Priya said she was Indian by heritage, Vivian brightened. She recalled *a friendly and very smart Indian visitor to her father's house* years ago — and remarked that Priya reminded her of him.
- She talked about her **first airplane trip** — Washington DC with her father, three days in the air, cigars, a sip of whiskey when nobody was watching.
- Her father was **Dr. Erik Loewe.** Ran a small team at Bodie, 1942–1948, under War Department cover. His phrase for the real work: *"listen to how the world keeps its books."* He died 1961 asking her to keep it private; she has, for 65 years.
- She spent summers at Bodie as a child. Mostly boring, sometimes beautiful. There was **one other kid one summer — "Little Jimmy" —** whose family camped near the town. And there were **crows** — always crows on the fence, watching her father. She thought they were smart.
- Correspondents her father wrote to: **Bell, MIT, "the Macy meetings."** Nobody in the party asked her for specific names, so she didn't offer any.
- Dakota asked whether she recognized **Huey Long.** She said flatly that she credits her longevity to never having paid attention to politics.
- She wasn't spiritual. Her father wasn't either. He believed in *observing*, not believing. She's come to agree.

**Mars slipped out during the conversation** to look around the house. Every sensitive door was locked. Not conspicuously — as a matter of decades of household habit. He came back empty-handed.

**Priya used a complaint about weak tea** to slide into a covert medical interview (taste changes, congestion, weakness), and checked Vivian's pulse in the process. Vivian's pulse was surprisingly strong and steady for 98.

**Vivian, at the notebook handoff, said quietly to Dakota:** *"You remind me of a man who used to visit my father."* She didn't finish the thought.

## The notebook

Vivian took you into her office and unlocked *the blue cabinet.* Inside was a shoebox with her father's leather journal. She handed it over. She said her father had cut pages out of it himself, near the end of his life — she'd always respected his privacy and never looked. Mira noticed the excised pages but Dakota kept the notebook for now; **a closer read will have to wait for next time.**

She said she'd been waiting for years for this moment. She recognized Dakota's surname from the mining-company ledger her father protected — that was the sign.

Almost as an afterthought, she named **Rosa Vega, Berkeley** — someone still alive who might know what her father was actually thinking. The party immediately recognized the surname: *the ranger.* Nobody needed to spell out what that meant.

On the way out, she spoke to Priya more quietly — *"there have always been people who ask about my father…"* — and offered to look through some other things she might have if you came back.

**Morgan noticed a wind-up mantel clock** and asked about it. Vivian said offhandedly that she hasn't touched it since her husband's death.

## The drive home

We ran out of time before the drive back. On the way to the car, Morgan's phone buzzed. It was **Ranger Vega:** *"see you in Berkeley soon."*

Session ends there.

## Where things stand

You aren't actively angry at Dakota anymore, but you're markedly warier of the way he talks around things. You saw him quiet and careful with Vivian — genuinely tender, in the moment — and you don't know yet whether *that* was the persona or the loud version is. You seem to have a common interest with him. You don't know yet whether you can trust him.

Vivian's father's notebook is with Dakota. There's more Vivian might have. Rosa Vega — and the ranger — are Berkeley now.

Session 5 is up to you.
